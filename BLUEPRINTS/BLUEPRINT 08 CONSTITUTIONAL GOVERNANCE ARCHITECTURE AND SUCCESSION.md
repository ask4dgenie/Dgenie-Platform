__A NOTE ON THIS MERGER__

Four documents become one here because all four answer the same underlying question at different resolutions: who holds authority inside DGENIE, and how does that authority survive contact with time. Governance named the offices and the rules binding them. The Board of Stewards Charter gave one of those offices, the Board, its actual operating bylaws. Succession & Leadership Development named how every office, the Board included, replaces itself without ever depending on one irreplaceable person. And the Master Architecture Map, DGENIE's original meta-blueprint, is the map showing how every other document in this Charter fits together, a map that belongs here rather than standing alone, since governance is exactly the function that has to hold the whole architecture accountable to itself.

This document is organized into four labeled Parts rather than fully interleaved, because the four source documents sit at different points inside the very amendment-tier system Part One below describes: Governance Framework content sits at the Constitutional Blueprint tier, the Board Charter and Succession Framework sit at the Governance Blueprint tier, and the Architecture Map is, by its own description, a map rather than a source of new principle. Merging their words into one continuous prose would blur a distinction the architecture itself depends on. Keeping them as four clearly labeled Parts inside one document achieves Duncan's instruction to consolidate without erasing that internal structure.

# PART ONE — CONSTITUTIONAL GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

__The Constitutional Guardians of the DGENIE Charter__

__PURPOSE OF THIS PART__

This Part does not manage daily operations. It exists to preserve the founding vision, protect the constitutional charter, govern authority, establish stewardship, and ensure that no future leader, board, technology, government, or commercial interest can fundamentally redefine DGENIE without following the constitutional processes named here.

__THE CONSTITUTIONAL DISCIPLINE RULE__

Every future document proposed anywhere in the DGENIE Charter is classified as exactly one of five kinds. A __Constitutional Blueprint__ establishes enduring principle. A __Governance Blueprint__ protects the Constitution through structure and process. A __Framework Blueprint__ interprets the Constitution into a working system within one domain. An __Operational Manual__ implements a Framework into procedure. A __Technical Specification__ implements an Operational Manual into a buildable system. No layer may contradict the layer above it, and no gap in a lower layer is ever solved by expanding a higher one. This Part is itself governance-classified: it protects and structures the Constitution rather than adding new foundational principle to it, and every gap it surfaces is recorded, never silently absorbed into its own text.

__FOUNDATIONAL GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES__

__DGENIE exists to serve learners, families, and communities, not itself.__ No governance decision may be justified primarily by what benefits the institution over the people it exists for.

__Every office is an office of stewardship.__ No position defined in Part One, Section Three is property. It is a trust, held temporarily and returned intact, or strengthened, to whoever holds it next.

__Authority exists to protect purpose.__ Power granted anywhere in this document is granted for a reason named in this document; authority exercised for any other reason has exceeded its mandate.

__Leadership is entrusted, not possessed.__ A title confers responsibility before it confers privilege, and the responsibility does not end when the privilege becomes inconvenient.

__The Constitution is supreme over every office.__ No office created by this document, including the Founder's, stands above the Charter that created it.

__No leader is above the Constitution.__ This is not a metaphor. Every accountability mechanism in Section Seven and every dispute principle in Section Eight applies to every office named in Section Three without exception.

__Every generation receives the vision in trust and passes it on strengthened.__ Succession, governed in full in Part Three below, is not an ending. It is the mechanism by which the Vision outlives any single steward.

__Truth, transparency, and accountability preserve trust.__ Consistent with Blueprint 07's Covenant of Trust, governance itself must be trustworthy, or it has no standing to ask trust of anyone else.

__Technology serves constitutional principles, not the reverse.__ No system, workflow, or AI agent mapped in Part Four below may be permitted to shape governance in a direction the Constitution did not authorize.

## SECTION ONE — CONSTITUTIONAL SUPREMACY

The Constitutional Charter is the highest governing authority of DGENIE. No office, technology, revenue opportunity, or external pressure outranks it.

```
VISION
  (Blueprint 01 — supreme and unamendable in substance)
    ↓
MISSION
  (Blueprint 01 — the Vision's working expression)
    ↓
CONSTITUTION
  (Blueprints 01 through 15)
    ↓
GOVERNANCE
  (This document's offices, authorities, and processes)
    ↓
FRAMEWORKS
  (Domain-specific systems interpreting the Constitution)
    ↓
OPERATIONAL MANUALS
  (Procedures implementing a Framework under local law and context)
    ↓
TECHNICAL SYSTEMS
  (The buildable platform mapped in Part Four below)
```

No lower layer may contradict a higher layer. A Technical System that conflicts with an Operational Manual is wrong. An Operational Manual that conflicts with a Framework is wrong. A Framework that conflicts with the Constitution is void regardless of who approved it. Governance itself, this Part, is bound by the same rule: nothing in Sections Two through Twelve below may contradict any other Blueprint in this Charter, and where ambiguity exists, the earlier, more foundational blueprint governs the interpretation.

## SECTION TWO — STEWARDSHIP PHILOSOPHY

DGENIE replaces ownership with stewardship as its defining governance concept. No individual owns the mission, not the Founder, not a future Chief Executive, not an investor, not a government partner. Every leader temporarily stewards it, for a season, on behalf of the learners the mission exists to serve, and hands it forward accountable to how they held it.

Stewardship differs from ownership in three ways that govern everything else in this Part. An owner may do as they wish with what they own; a steward may only act within the mandate entrusted to them, defined office by office in Section Three. An owner's authority ends only at their choosing; a steward's authority is always subject to the accountability mechanisms in Section Seven and can be withdrawn through the processes in Part Three and Section Eight. An owner benefits personally from what they hold; a steward's success is measured by the condition in which they leave the mission for the next steward, not by personal gain extracted along the way.

This is not merely a legal structure. It is the cultural principle that must be visible in how every office-holder in Section Three actually behaves, and it is the standard every Constitutional Steward affirms in the Oath in Section Twelve.

## SECTION THREE — CONSTITUTIONAL OFFICES

Twelve offices exist to protect DGENIE. None exists for its own sake; each is justified only by the specific protective function it serves.

__Founder.__ Purpose: originates and safeguards the Vision itself. Authority: final interpretive voice on the Vision's meaning, exercised through the Constitutional Council rather than unilaterally once that Council is seated. Limitations: bound by every principle in this document exactly as any other office; may not amend a Foundational Principle alone, per Section Five. Responsibilities: ensures every blueprint remains faithful to Blueprint 01. Accountability: to the Constitutional Council and, in matters of safeguarding, to the Chief Safeguarding Officer's independent authority under Section Four. Relationship to other offices: convenes the Founding Council; does not sit above the Constitutional Council once succession, per Part Three below, has occurred.

__Founding Council.__ Purpose: the original body of architects and early leaders who carried DGENIE from Vision into working Constitution. Authority: advisory and historical; its members carry institutional memory rather than binding present-day authority once the Constitutional Council is fully formed. Its one bounded exception, described in Part Three, Section Six, is affirming the Founder's nominations to the first Constitutional Council; once that Council is seated, this exception is spent and does not recur. Limitations: cannot override the Constitutional Council or Board of Stewards. Responsibilities: preserves the record of original intent referenced whenever a future ambiguity requires it. Accountability: to the Constitution itself. Relationship to other offices: feeds institutional memory into succession planning under Part Three below.

__Constitutional Council.__ Purpose: the sole guardian of the Charter's integrity. Authority: reviews and must approve any amendment classified under Section Five as touching a Major Article or Foundational Principle; conducts the Constitutional and Architectural Integrity Reviews named in Section Ten. Limitations: cannot itself operate the institution day to day, and cannot amend a Foundational Principle without meeting the highest bar defined in Section Five. Responsibilities: the ultimate check against any office, including the Founder's, drifting from the Constitution. Accountability: to the Constitution and, structurally, to the community's trust named in Blueprint 07. Relationship to other offices: reviews and can pause any decision from the Board of Stewards or any Chief that appears to violate this Charter. Composition: seven members; its initial formation and the transition to a permanent nomination process are defined in Part Three, Section Six.

__Board of Stewards.__ Purpose: holds fiduciary, not proprietary, responsibility for the whole institution. Accountability: to the Constitutional Council on matters of Charter fidelity, and to the community on matters of transparency under Section Seven. Relationship to other offices: appoints the Chief Executive; does not directly manage the four Chief offices below. Its full operating bylaws are given in Part Two of this document.

## SECTION FOUR — SEPARATION OF AUTHORITY

Six distinct authorities exist so that no single office can concentrate unchecked power over the whole institution.

__Constitutional Authority__ belongs to the Constitutional Council: the power to interpret the Charter and approve or block amendments under Section Five.

__Educational Authority__ belongs to the Chief Educational Architect: the power to shape curriculum and methodology within the bounds Blueprints 01 through 05 already set.

__Technology Authority__ belongs to the Chief Technology Architect: the power to build and govern the platform and AI ecosystem within the bounds of Section Nine.

__Financial Authority__ belongs to the Board of Stewards: the power to allocate resources and hold the institution to account for its stewardship of them.

__Safeguarding Authority__ belongs to the Chief Safeguarding Officer, held with the structural independence named in Section Three: the power to act, escalate, and pause anything else in the institution when a learner's safety or trust is at risk.

__Community Authority__ belongs to the Chief Community Officer, Regional Stewards, and Local Community Leaders together: the power to shape the relational life of the community across its seven layers.

Mutual accountability follows directly from this separation. The Chief Educational Architect cannot silence a Safeguarding concern about a curriculum decision. The Chief Technology Architect cannot deploy an AI feature the Chief Safeguarding Officer has flagged. The Board of Stewards cannot spend the institution into a position that compromises the mission the Constitutional Council protects. No single authority, including the Chief Executive's coordinating role, can override another authority's domain; disagreements between authorities are resolved through the dispute principles in Section Eight, never through one office simply outranking another.

## SECTION FIVE — CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT PROCESS

Not every change to the DGENIE Charter requires the same process. Five tiers exist, from nearly immutable to routine.

__Foundational Principles.__ The Vision Statement in Blueprint 01, the Foundational Declaration in Blueprint 07, the Educational Red Lines in Blueprint 04, and the Constitutional Red Lines in Blueprint 07 sit at this tier. These are nearly immutable because they are not features of DGENIE; they are what makes DGENIE DGENIE. Amending one does not evolve the institution, it replaces it with a different institution operating under the same name. Amendment at this tier requires unanimous Constitutional Council approval, a Board of Stewards supermajority, and a mandatory public reflection period before ratification.

__Major Articles.__ The core structure of an existing blueprint, such as the Two-Track Delivery Model in Blueprint 04 or the seven-layer community structure in Blueprint 06. Amendment requires Constitutional Council approval and Board of Stewards supermajority, without the mandatory reflection period required above.

__Framework Amendments.__ Adjustments within a Framework that do not alter its founding structure, such as updating a Genius Level's evidence bar or adding a new curriculum domain. Amendment requires Constitutional Council review and the relevant Chief's formal proposal.

__Operational Revisions.__ Changes to how a Framework is implemented, not what it establishes. Authority rests with the relevant Chief, with notification, not approval, required from the Constitutional Council.

__Technical Changes.__ Implementation-level changes to the platform that do not touch AI governance boundaries or data stewardship principles. Authority rests with the Chief Technology Architect alone.

This tiering exists so that the Constitution can remain alive and improvable without ever becoming unstable. A curriculum domain can be added without a constitutional crisis; the Vision cannot be quietly rewritten through the same door.

## SECTION SIX — LEADERSHIP SUCCESSION (PRINCIPLES)

DGENIE is built to outlive every individual who currently leads it, including its Founder. This Section states the governing principles; Part Three of this document gives them full operational form.

__Founder succession.__ The Founder's authority does not pass by inheritance or personal appointment. It passes through the Constitutional Council, which affirms a successor's fitness against the same standard, demonstrated stewardship, character, and fidelity to the Vision, that governs every other office.

__Institutional memory.__ The Founding Council and the Legacy Archive together preserve the record of original intent, so that no future leader must guess at what a founding decision actually meant.

__Leadership formation.__ Future stewards are not recruited from outside the mission's own life. The lifelong membership journey mapped in Blueprint 06, Learner through Legacy Steward, is the actual pipeline that produces future Mentors, Leaders, and, in time, Chiefs and Council members.

__Selection principles.__ Every office in Section Three is filled on the basis of demonstrated stewardship and character, measured against the Graduate Profile's own pillars, never on ambition, tenure, or personal loyalty to a predecessor.

__Transition.__ Every succession includes a defined overlap period and a documented handoff, so that continuity of care for learners is never interrupted by a change in office.

__Continuity.__ What continues across every succession is the Constitution itself, not any individual. This is the entire purpose of Section One's supremacy chain: no successor inherits unchecked power, only stewardship bound by the same Charter their predecessor was bound by.

DGENIE deliberately avoids personality-driven governance. An office that depends on one irreplaceable person has already failed the stewardship philosophy in Section Two.

## SECTION SEVEN — ACCOUNTABILITY & TRANSPARENCY

__Integrity.__ Every office-holder's private conduct is expected to match their public role, consistent with Blueprint 07's Community Standards.

__Financial stewardship.__ Resources entrusted to DGENIE are used only for the mission, documented honestly, and never treated as personal benefit for any office-holder.

__Decision transparency.__ Major decisions are explainable in plain language to the community they affect, consistent with the transparency principles already established for AI in Blueprint 04 and extended here to human governance.

__Conflict of interest.__ Any office-holder whose personal interest could compromise a decision declares it and steps back from that decision, exactly as required of Mentors in Blueprint 07.

__Independent review.__ The most consequential decisions, and every safeguarding matter, are reviewed by someone outside the immediate chain of command, per the Constitutional Guardianship reviews in Section Ten and the independent body established in Blueprint 09.

__Regular constitutional audits.__ The Constitutional Council conducts periodic reviews, named in Section Ten, testing whether the institution as actually operating still matches the Charter as written.

__Public trust.__ DGENIE's legitimacy rests on the community's confidence that this document is actually being followed, not merely published.

__Community participation.__ Families, learners, and members have real channels, through Local Community Leaders and Advisory Councils, to raise concerns and shape governance, not merely to be informed of decisions after the fact.

__Protection for those who raise concerns in good faith.__ Consistent with Blueprint 07's Red Line against retaliation, no office-holder may be penalized for raising a legitimate concern about any other office, including their own superior.

## SECTION EIGHT — CONSTITUTIONAL DISPUTE RESOLUTION

This Section governs disputes about governance and constitutional authority. Where a dispute is actually a safeguarding concern, the principles in Blueprint 07 govern instead, not this Section.

__Listening.__ Every party to a governance dispute is heard fully before any conclusion is reached.

__Evidence.__ Decisions are made on documented fact, not on the seniority or persuasiveness of whoever speaks last.

__Fairness.__ Every office-holder, regardless of rank, receives the same standard of process.

__Restoration where possible.__ Where a dispute can be resolved in a way that repairs the relationship and strengthens the institution, that outcome is preferred over one that merely assigns blame.

__Appeals.__ A decision by any office below the Constitutional Council can be appealed to it.

__Independent review.__ The most serious governance disputes, particularly those involving the Chief Executive, Board of Stewards, or Founder, are reviewed by parties outside the dispute itself, per Blueprint 09.

__Protection against abuse of authority.__ No office may use its authority to punish a party for having raised the dispute in the first place.

__Mission-first decision making.__ Every governance dispute is ultimately resolved by asking which outcome best protects the learners, families, and communities DGENIE exists to serve, not which outcome most benefits any office-holder personally.

## SECTION NINE — GOVERNANCE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

This Section addresses AI's relationship to constitutional authority specifically, distinct from the conduct-level rules already established in Blueprint 04 and Blueprint 07, which govern how an AI agent behaves toward a learner. This Section governs whether an AI agent may ever hold power inside the institution itself, and the answer is no.

__AI shall never possess constitutional authority.__ No AI agent may hold any office named in Section Three, cast a governance vote, or approve a constitutional amendment at any tier defined in Section Five.

__AI may advise.__ An agent may surface information, patterns, or options relevant to a governance decision.

__AI may analyze.__ An agent may process data at the scale mapped in Part Four's Analytics Agents, always in aggregate, never as the deciding voice on an individual matter.

__AI may recommend.__ An agent may propose a course of action, always subject to human evaluation before it becomes a decision.

__AI may automate.__ An agent may execute the routine, already-decided steps of a workflow mapped in Part Four.

__AI shall never govern.__ No agent may be the final decision-maker on any matter touching authority, amendment, succession, or discipline of an office-holder.

Final responsibility always rests with accountable human stewards named in Section Three. This is not a temporary limitation awaiting more capable technology. It is a permanent constitutional boundary, because governance requires the kind of moral accountability, per Blueprint 07, that only a human steward can actually bear.

## SECTION TEN — CONSTITUTIONAL GUARDIANSHIP

Seven recurring reviews protect the Charter's fidelity to the founding Vision over time.

__Constitutional Reviews,__ conducted by the Constitutional Council, test whether recent decisions across the institution remain consistent with the whole Charter.

__Architectural Integrity Reviews,__ conducted by the Constitutional Council with the Chief Technology Architect, test whether the platform mapped in Part Four still matches the dependency graph established there.

__Mission Audits,__ conducted by the Chief Educational Architect, test whether the actual learner experience still produces the Graduate Profile Blueprint 01 describes.

__Safeguarding Audits,__ conducted by the Chief Safeguarding Officer with independent review from the body established in Blueprint 09, test compliance with every standard in Blueprint 07.

__Educational Audits,__ conducted by the Chief Educational Architect, test curriculum and methodology fidelity to Blueprints 01 through 05. __Extended 2026-08-15__ to include a sampled, foreknowledge-free re-triangulation of certified Genius Level advancements, per Blueprint 15 Part Two Section Seven, checking for the quiet grade-inflation drift a stretched Mentor's own caseload can produce without that Mentor ever consciously deciding to lower a bar — a check that does not depend on the certifying Mentor recognizing their own drift, since that recognition is exactly what the risk this addresses undermines.

__Technology Ethics Reviews,__ conducted jointly by the Chief Technology Architect and the Constitutional Council, test every AI agent against Section Nine of this document, Blueprint 07, and Blueprint 14.

__Community Trust Reviews,__ conducted by the Chief Community Officer with Advisory Council participation, test whether the community's actual confidence in DGENIE matches the Covenant of Trust in Blueprint 07.

__How this relates to Blueprint 09's parallel reviews, added 2026-08-14.__ Five of these seven, Mission Audits, Safeguarding Audits, Educational Audits, Technology Ethics Reviews, and Community Trust Reviews, test the same five domains Blueprint 09 Part Six's independent Oversight Body also reviews. That is deliberate, not duplicated effort, and this session's structural audit is the reason the distinction is now stated rather than left implicit. These five, run by the Chief responsible for the domain, test operational execution: whether the process for delivering on that domain is actually running as designed, day to day, catching drift early enough to correct it without waiting for an external cycle. Blueprint 09's parallel reviews test something different: whether the lived outcome for a learner or the community actually matches what the Charter promises, checked by a body with no operational stake in a flattering answer. A domain can pass its operational audit here and still fail its outcome review there, if the process is being followed correctly but no longer actually serves what the Charter requires; that divergence is itself a finding worth having, not evidence that one of the two reviews is redundant.

Together, these seven reviews are how DGENIE checks its own pulse, regularly and honestly, rather than waiting for a crisis to reveal that fidelity to the founding Vision has quietly eroded.

## SECTION ELEVEN — EXPANSION GOVERNANCE

Part Four, Section Eleven of this document establishes what stays constant and what adapts across nine expansion contexts. This Section establishes who approves such expansion.

Any expansion into a new context, any international expansion, regional federation, partner organization, future campus, or new digital community, requires joint approval from the Board of Stewards and the Constitutional Council before it proceeds. A Regional Steward holds delegated authority only for growth within an already-approved region; expansion into a new nation or context is never a purely operational decision.

Licensing, accreditation partnerships, and, if ever adopted, franchising, are each tested against the same standard: does this expansion still answer to the Graduate Profile, still protect Formation ahead of information, and still keep a human mentor, not a platform or a licensing fee, at the center of the relationship. Mission precedes growth in every case. No expansion may compromise constitutional integrity, and any growth opportunity that would require weakening a Foundational Principle to pursue is refused, regardless of its scale. The operational mechanics of licensing and expansion are carried by Blueprint 10.

## SECTION TWELVE — THE CONSTITUTIONAL STEWARD'S OATH

Every person entering one of the twelve offices named in Section Three affirms this oath before beginning their service.

I affirm faithfulness to the Vision: to raise generations who know who they are, discover why they were created, and use their gifts to transform their communities and the world.

I affirm faithfulness to the Mission: to discover, develop, and deploy the genius entrusted to every learner in my care.

I will protect learners above every other interest, including my own.

I will protect families as partners, never treating my authority as a substitute for theirs.

I will protect this Constitutional Charter, and I will never use my office to weaken what it protects.

I will exercise the authority entrusted to me wisely, remembering that I hold it in stewardship, not in possession.

I will lead with humility, knowing that my office existed before I held it and will continue after I am gone.

I will act with integrity, even when no one beyond this community would ever know if I did not.

I will serve the generations who will inherit this mission after me, and I will hand forward what was entrusted to me strengthened, not diminished.

__This is the oath of every Constitutional Steward of DGENIE.__

# PART TWO — THE BOARD OF STEWARDS CHARTER & BYLAWS

__PURPOSE OF THIS PART__

The Board of Stewards holds fiduciary responsibility for the whole institution. This Part defines its composition, appointment, terms, meetings, voting, committees, conflict-of-interest rules, and its relationship to the Constitutional Council and the Chief Executive. It is written to be adopted directly by a Board convened under Part One as its own governing instrument.

## SECTION ONE — NAME & AUTHORITY

The official name of this body is __DGENIE Model Schools — Board of Stewards__.

Its authority is derived from Part One, Section Three, which establishes the Board of Stewards as a Constitutional Office within the DGENIE Constitutional Charter. This Part does not create that authority; it operationalizes it.

__Supremacy clause.__ No action of the Board, whether a resolution, appointment, budget approval, or bylaw amendment, may violate the DGENIE Constitutional Charter. Where any provision of this Part conflicts with the Constitutional Charter, the Constitutional Charter prevails, and the conflicting provision is void to the extent of the conflict.

## SECTION TWO — PURPOSE & RESPONSIBILITIES

The Board holds fiduciary stewardship of all institutional resources, per Blueprint 10, exercising this stewardship through the specific powers named below rather than through direct operational control.

The Board __appoints, oversees, and may remove the Chief Executive__, per the process in Section Four.

The Board __approves major resource allocation, budgets, and financial plans__, exercising the Financial Authority established in Part One, Section Four.

The Board __oversees financial stewardship__ as a standing responsibility, per Blueprint 10, primarily through the Finance & Audit Committee named in Section Eight.

The Board __approves new partnerships, significant expansions, or any matter requiring Board authority__, applying the partnership principles established in Blueprint 10.

The Board __receives and acts upon findings from the Oversight Body__ established in Blueprint 09, treating every escalation as a matter requiring genuine Board attention, not procedural acknowledgment.

## SECTION THREE — COMPOSITION

The Board consists of between five and nine voting members, an odd number chosen so that no vote requiring a simple majority can end in a tie.

__Qualifications.__ Every member is chosen for integrity, relevant expertise in education, finance, governance, law, or technology, and demonstrated commitment to DGENIE's Vision as established in Blueprint 01. At least one member holds financial or accounting expertise. At least one member holds a safeguarding or child protection background, strengthening the Board's capacity to engage seriously with matters arising under Blueprint 07.

__Terms.__ Members serve staggered three-year terms, so that the Board's composition never turns over all at once, and may serve a maximum of two consecutive terms before a required interval away from the Board.

__Eligibility.__ No current operational employee of DGENIE may serve as a voting member, with one exception: the Chief Executive sits on the Board ex officio, without a vote, ensuring the Board hears directly from operational leadership without operational leadership governing itself.

__Founder's role.__ The Founder may serve in an ex officio, non-voting advisory capacity during DGENIE's first five years, after which this provision lapses unless renewed by Board amendment under Section Twelve.

## SECTION FOUR — APPOINTMENT & SUCCESSION

__Nomination.__ The current Board nominates candidates for new or renewing seats. Every appointment is confirmed by the Constitutional Council before taking effect, ensuring the Board's composition remains subject to a constitutional check beyond its own preference.

__Vacancies.__ A vacancy is filled within ninety days of arising. An interim appointment, made by the remaining Board and confirmed by the Constitutional Council on an expedited basis, may serve until the ordinary nomination process is complete.

__Removal.__ A member may be removed only for cause, meaning gross negligence, a proven conflict of interest left undisclosed, or a constitutional violation. Removal requires a supermajority vote of the full Board, defined in Section Seven, and is subject to review by the Constitutional Council before taking final effect, preserving the removed member's right to a fair process consistent with the natural justice principle established in Blueprint 09.

## SECTION FIVE — OFFICERS OF THE BOARD

The Board elects, annually, from among its voting members: a __Chair__, who presides over meetings and represents the Board in its dealings with the Chief Executive and the Constitutional Council, a non-executive role carrying no authority beyond that of any other voting member outside of presiding; a __Vice-Chair__, who presides in the Chair's absence and supports the Chair's duties; a __Secretary__, responsible for the accuracy and archival of minutes under Section Six; and a __Treasurer__, who works most closely with the Finance & Audit Committee named in Section Eight on the Board's behalf.

No officer may serve in the same office for more than three consecutive annual terms, ensuring the same discipline against entrenchment that governs Board membership generally applies to its leadership.

## SECTION SIX — MEETINGS

The Board meets no less than quarterly, in person or virtually, consistent with DGENIE's digital-first nature established in Blueprint 06. Emergency meetings may be called with twenty-four hours' notice where a matter cannot reasonably wait for the next scheduled meeting.

__Quorum__ is a majority of voting members. __Proxy voting is not permitted__; a member's vote reflects their own genuine deliberation, present at the meeting where the matter is discussed, not a delegated instruction.

Minutes are kept for every meeting, approved at the following meeting, and archived as part of Institutional Memory, per Blueprint 13. Archived minutes are available to the Constitutional Council and the Oversight Body at all times, per Section Ten.

## SECTION SEVEN — VOTING & DECISION-MAKING

__Ordinary decisions__ require a majority of those present and voting, provided quorum is met.

__Major decisions__, specifically budget approval, appointment or removal of the Chief Executive, proposed constitutional amendments originating from the Board, and significant institutional expansions, require a __supermajority__: two-thirds of all voting members, not merely those present.

Voting records are kept in full for internal transparency and are anonymized when published in the public reports named in Section Ten, balancing the Transparency principle established in Part One with the privacy every individual member is entitled to in ordinary deliberation.

## SECTION EIGHT — COMMITTEES

The Board maintains three mandatory standing committees.

The __Finance & Audit Committee__ oversees budget preparation, reserve levels, and financial controls, working directly beneath the Financial Stewardship principles in Blueprint 10 and reporting its findings to the full Board.

The __Governance & Nominating Committee__ oversees Board composition, succession planning consistent with Part Three of this document, and periodic Board self-evaluation.

The __Safeguarding & Ethics Committee__ receives concerns escalated from the Oversight Body under Blueprint 09 and ensures they receive genuine Board-level attention. This committee does not re-investigate a matter the Oversight Body has already examined; its role is to ensure the Board acts on what the Oversight Body found, preserving the Oversight Body's independence.

The Board may form ad hoc committees as circumstances require. Every committee, standing or ad hoc, operates under a defined terms of reference, meets no less than quarterly, and reports formally to the full Board.

## SECTION NINE — CONFLICT OF INTEREST

Every Board member discloses any personal, financial, or relational interest that could reasonably appear to affect their judgment on a matter before the Board, at the point the matter arises, not only at the annual declaration described below.

A member with a disclosed conflict recuses themselves from discussion and voting on that matter entirely.

Every member files an __annual conflict-of-interest declaration__ with the Oversight Body, per Blueprint 09, giving the Oversight Body's independent review actual visibility into the Board's own conflict management.

Failure to disclose a genuine conflict, once discovered, results in immediate removal, without the ninety-day grace period that governs an ordinary vacancy under Section Four.

## SECTION TEN — ACCOUNTABILITY & TRANSPARENCY

The Board publishes an __Annual Report__ to the community, summarizing financial stewardship and key decisions of the year, in keeping with the Accountability & Transparency principle established in Part One.

The Board submits to regular __Constitutional Audits__ conducted by the Oversight Body, per Blueprint 09, and treats an audit finding with the same seriousness the Board expects of every office it oversees.

Board minutes, per Section Six, are archived and made available to the Constitutional Council and the Oversight Body at all times, not merely on request.

## SECTION ELEVEN — RELATIONSHIP WITH OTHER OFFICES

__Board to Constitutional Council.__ The Board submits major decisions to the Constitutional Council for review of constitutional fidelity but does not require Council approval for ordinary operational matters within its own authority.

__Board to Chief Executive.__ The Board appoints the Chief Executive, evaluates their performance annually, and may remove them for cause under the process the Board itself defines consistent with Section Four's standard. The Board delegates day-to-day operational authority to the Chief Executive but retains ultimate fiduciary responsibility for the institution at all times; delegation of authority is never delegation of accountability.

__Board to Oversight Body.__ The Board receives the Oversight Body's reports, acts on its recommendations, and is itself subject to the Oversight Body's audits, per Blueprint 09. No provision of this Part exempts the Board from that review.

## SECTION TWELVE — AMENDMENT OF THIS CHARTER

Amendments to this Part require a two-thirds vote of the full Board and ratification by the Constitutional Council, applying the Major Article tier of the Amendment Process established in Part One, Section Five. No amendment may take effect, and no provision of this Part may be read, in a way that conflicts with the Constitutional Charter, per the supremacy clause in Section One.

# PART THREE — SUCCESSION & LEADERSHIP DEVELOPMENT FRAMEWORK

## SECTION ONE — PURPOSE & SCOPE

This Part governs succession and leadership development for every constitutional office named in Part One, Section Three: the Founder, the Founding Council, the Constitutional Council, the Board of Stewards, the Chief Executive, the Chief Educational Architect, the Chief Technology Architect, the Chief Safeguarding Officer, the Chief Community Officer, Regional Stewards, Local Community Leaders, and Advisory Councils.

It also applies to key operational roles not named as constitutional offices but critical to mission continuity: Mentors, holding every learner's whole-child relationship in one role rather than split across specialists, per Blueprint 11; Community Mentors, in their distinct governance capacity as a Local Learning Circle's accountable leader, named as a constitutional office above; Teachers, bound to the Home Track and to subject-specific instruction, per Blueprint 04 and Blueprint 11; Learning Coaches; and Administrators.

This Part does not replace day-to-day hiring and performance management. It establishes the constitutional pipeline through which leadership is formed and transitioned, leaving ordinary employment practice to operational policy.

## SECTION TWO — FOUNDATIONAL SUCCESSION PRINCIPLES

This Section extends, without redefining, the Leadership Succession principle established in Part One, Section Six.

No office depends on any single individual; DGENIE is built to outlive every leader who serves it. Succession is planned, not reactive; every office carries a documented succession plan before it is ever needed. Leadership is formed intentionally, not discovered by accident, through the pipeline described in Section Three. Selection is based on demonstrated stewardship and character, measured against the Graduate Profile established in Blueprint 01, never on tenure alone. Every transition includes a defined overlap period and a documented handoff, per Section Ten. Mission continuity is the overriding priority; no succession decision may jeopardize the Vision established in Blueprint 01, however urgent the vacancy.

## SECTION THREE — THE LEADERSHIP PIPELINE

This Part builds directly on the nine-stage Lifelong Membership Journey established in Blueprint 06: Learner, Builder, Contributor, Mentor, Leader, Parent, Investor, Architect, and Legacy Steward. Leadership formation is not a discrete event that begins the day someone enters a constitutional office; it is a continuous process that begins in childhood, moving through every stage of that journey.

At each stage, leadership capacity is observed, tested, and intentionally developed, per Section Four. No one enters a constitutional office without having demonstrated faithfulness at a lower level of responsibility first; a person who has not been faithful as a Contributor is not considered for a role that assumes the faithfulness of a Leader.

The pipeline is transparent. Potential successors are known, observed, and developed openly over time, not identified suddenly at the moment a vacancy arises.

## SECTION FOUR — SUCCESSION PLANNING REQUIREMENTS

Every constitutional office maintains a documented succession plan, reviewed annually by the Board of Stewards and the Constitutional Council.

Each plan identifies at least two potential successors, whether internal or external; the development path for each; a timeline for transition, covering both a planned and an emergency scenario; the knowledge, skills, and character required for the office; and the handoff process, including overlap, documentation, and introduction to stakeholders, per Section Ten.

__Emergency succession.__ If an office becomes vacant unexpectedly, an interim successor is designated within forty-eight hours, and a permanent successor is confirmed within ninety days, per the full process detailed in Section Eleven.

## SECTION FIVE — LEADERSHIP DEVELOPMENT FRAMEWORK

__Observation.__ Leadership potential is observed through the Lifelong Membership Journey and through demonstrated faithfulness in a person's current role, not through self-nomination or ambition alone.

__Formation.__ Potential leaders are intentionally formed through mentorship by current office-holders; exposure to the constitutional reasoning behind institutional decisions, per the Institutional Memory architecture in Blueprint 13; responsibility in progressively more significant roles; and reflective practice and accountability, consistent with the Reflection principle established in Blueprint 04.

__Testing.__ Potential leaders are given real responsibility with real consequences, observed over time, and evaluated against the Graduate Profile established in Blueprint 01, not against a hypothetical standard divorced from actual performance.

__Selection.__ Selection is based on demonstrated character, stewardship, and fidelity to the Vision, never on tenure, ambition, or personal loyalty to a predecessor. A candidate who has cultivated influence rather than character is not, by this standard, a suitable successor regardless of their visibility.

## SECTION SIX — FOUNDER SUCCESSION

This Section is the most sensitive in this Part and is treated with particular care, since it governs the transition away from the person whose vision founded the institution.

The Founder's authority does not pass by inheritance or personal appointment. A successor is affirmed by the Constitutional Council, a constitutional safeguard against DGENIE ever becoming dependent on a single individual or bloodline, per Part One, Section Six. The criteria are the same as for every other office under this Part: demonstrated stewardship, character, and fidelity to the Vision, tested through the pipeline in Section Three rather than assumed from proximity to the Founder.

The Founder may serve in an advisory capacity after succession, but without binding authority, an arrangement consistent with the ex officio, non-voting advisory role already established for the Founder's first five years on the Board of Stewards in Part Two, Section Three.

The Founder's succession plan is the highest-priority succession plan in the institution and receives the Constitutional Council's direct, ongoing attention, not merely its annual review.

__Constitutional Council succession and initial formation.__

The Constitutional Council's own membership cannot be seated by the Constitutional Council itself without becoming self-perpetuating, a closed loop this Blueprint forbids everywhere else and cannot allow here either. Its very first seating is therefore an explicit, bounded exception, not an oversight, and this Part is where that exception is written down rather than left implied.

__Initial formation.__ The first Constitutional Council is seated by the Founder, exercising the interim authority described in Part One, Section Three's Founder entry, authority that exists only for the period before a Council exists to channel it through. The Founder nominates seven initial members, chosen for the independence, integrity, and relevant expertise named for the Oversight Body in Blueprint 09, Part Five, and presents those nominations to the Founding Council for affirmation. That affirmation is the one binding exercise of the Founding Council's authority contemplated in Part One, Section Three; once given, the Founding Council's role reverts fully to the advisory and historical function described there.

__Initial terms.__ Founder-seated members serve staggered terms, two members at two years, two at four years, and three at six years, establishing the staggered rotation the permanent model below inherits so the Council is never fully replaced at once.

__Transition to the permanent model.__ This Founder-seeded arrangement is time-bound, not standing practice. Once verified active DGENIE membership, counted across every category defined in Blueprint 06, Part Four, reaches one thousand members, the Founder-seeding authority above lapses permanently, whether or not every Founder-seated term has expired. From that point forward, a vacancy on the Constitutional Council is filled by nomination from the Board of Stewards' Governance & Nominating Committee, per Part Two, Section Eight, and confirmed by a two-thirds vote of the sitting Constitutional Council, keeping the nominating and confirming bodies distinct so the Council is never asked to fill its own vacancy unassisted. A Founder-seated member whose term has not yet expired when the threshold is reached continues to serve out that term under the original arrangement; only the appointment of new members changes.

__Why the Founder, not the Founding Council, seeds the first Council.__ Making the Founding Council the appointing body would only relocate the bootstrap problem one level up, since the Founding Council's own membership is nowhere formally constituted either. The Founder is a single, already-defined office with no equivalent regress problem, and Part One, Section Three already contemplates the Founder holding authority not yet channeled through a Council. Naming that authority explicitly, and bounding it with a hard numeric transition trigger, closes the gap without inventing a new office.

## SECTION SEVEN — BOARD OF STEWARDS SUCCESSION

Board members serve staggered terms, per Part Two, Section Three. New members are nominated by the Board's Governance & Nominating Committee, per Part Two, Section Eight, and confirmed by the Constitutional Council, per Part Two, Section Four.

Succession is continuous, not episodic: a vacancy is filled within ninety days, exactly as Part Two, Section Four requires. The Board Chair is elected annually, per Part Two, Section Five, with the term limits established there preventing entrenchment in that role specifically.

The Board's own succession health is reviewed annually as part of its governance responsibilities, a standing agenda item for the Governance & Nominating Committee rather than a matter raised only when a vacancy is imminent.

## SECTION EIGHT — CHIEF EXECUTIVE SUCCESSION

The Chief Executive is appointed by the Board of Stewards, per Part Two, Section Eleven. The succession plan for this office is maintained by the Board's Governance & Nominating Committee.

Internal candidates are developed through the leadership pipeline described in Section Three; external candidates are considered only where no internal candidate is suitable, preserving the pipeline's integrity as the primary route to this office rather than treating it as a formality.

Transition includes a defined overlap with the outgoing Chief Executive, a documented handoff per Section Ten, and a deliberate introduction to key stakeholders, families, mentors, partners, and community leaders alike, so that the transition is felt as continuity rather than disruption.

## SECTION NINE — CHIEF OFFICER SUCCESSION

Each Chief Officer, the Chief Educational Architect, Chief Technology Architect, Chief Safeguarding Officer, and Chief Community Officer, maintains a succession plan, reviewed annually by the Chief Executive and the Board's Governance & Nominating Committee.

Internal candidates are developed through the leadership pipeline, and successors are identified no less than two years before an anticipated transition, giving genuine time for the formation described in Section Five rather than a hurried handoff.

__Emergency succession__ for a Chief Officer follows the same forty-eight-hour interim designation required across every office under this Part, per Section Eleven.

Regional Stewards are appointed by the Chief Community Officer, with Board confirmation. Local Community Leaders are appointed by their Regional Steward, with Chief Community Officer confirmation. Local succession is managed at the community level, with oversight from the layer immediately above it. Succession plans for every Local Community Leader position are maintained at the Regional level, ensuring no single community's leadership gap goes unnoticed by the wider institution.

## SECTION TEN — INSTITUTIONAL KNOWLEDGE TRANSFER

Succession includes the deliberate transfer of institutional knowledge, not merely the transfer of a title. This Section gives direct operational effect to the Institutional Memory architecture established in Blueprint 13.

Every outgoing office-holder documents the reasoning behind their key decisions, not merely the decisions themselves; their active partnerships and relationships, per Blueprint 10; any unresolved issues and risks; and their strategic priorities and long-term plans. This documentation is stored in the Institutional Memory system established in Blueprint 13, becoming part of the institution's own accumulated wisdom rather than remaining in one person's private knowledge.

Overlap periods, required by Section Two for every transition, are structured to enable active knowledge transfer, working sessions, joint decisions, and direct introduction to relationships, not passive observation alone. A successor who has only watched their predecessor work has not yet received what this Section requires.

## SECTION ELEVEN — EMERGENCY SUCCESSION

An unexpected vacancy in any office named in Section One triggers the designation of an interim successor within forty-eight hours, drawn from the succession plan required under Section Four wherever possible.

A permanent successor is confirmed within ninety days, following the same selection standard set out in Section Five, not a lowered standard adopted for the sake of speed.

The Board of Stewards and Constitutional Council are notified immediately upon any emergency succession. Where the vacancy carries any safeguarding implication, it is escalated per Blueprint 07's crisis principles without delay. The Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09 is notified and may review the emergency transition as part of its ordinary oversight function.

## SECTION TWELVE — MONITORING AND REVIEW

Succession plans are reviewed annually by the Board's Governance & Nominating Committee. The Constitutional Council reviews succession plans for every constitutional office as part of its own standing responsibility.

The Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09 may audit succession practices as part of its Constitutional Audit, treating the presence or absence of a current succession plan as a legitimate audit finding.

Failure to maintain a current succession plan for any office is treated as a governance risk, reportable to the Board of Stewards exactly as any other governance risk would be.

## SECTION THIRTEEN — AMENDMENT OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Amendments to this Part require a proposal from the Chief Executive and approval by the Board of Stewards, applying the Framework Amendments tier of the Amendment Process established in Part One, Section Five.

## SECTION ELEVEN — FUTURE EXPANSION FRAMEWORK

The architecture is built to extend into new contexts without altering its constitutional foundation. In every case below, the Vision, the Graduate Profile, Formation, Genius Discovery, the Five Lifelong Competencies, and the Genius Level system remain unchanged. What adapts is delivery, exactly as the Home Track and Membership Track already demonstrate two different deliveries of one unchanged methodology.

__Physical Schools.__ A campus becomes, structurally, a very large and permanent Local Learning Circle.

__Homeschool Networks.__ Structurally identical to the existing Home Track.

__Partner Schools.__ An existing school licenses the Curriculum and Methodology while retaining its own campus and staff.

__Universities.__ The Genius Level system extends naturally past Level 12.

__Corporate Learning.__ The Five Lifelong Competencies and the Contribution Principle apply directly to adult professional formation.

__Church Training.__ The Spiritual Formation dimension and the Mentor's spiritual-formation responsibility, per Blueprint 11, extend directly.

__Community Development.__ The Local Learning Circle and Community Impact Project structures extend directly into neighborhoods with no formal DGENIE learners yet.

__Government Partnerships.__ Regional and National Community layers already anticipate this.

__International Federations.__ The Global Community layer already anticipates this.

In every one of these nine contexts, the test is the same: does this expansion still answer to the Graduate Profile, still protect Formation ahead of information, and still keep a human mentor, not a platform, at the center of the relationship. The operational mechanics of each context are carried forward by Blueprint 10.

## SECTION TWELVE — THE CONSTITUTIONAL ARCHITECTURE DECLARATION

Every future policy, AI agent, workflow, platform feature, curriculum, campus, partnership, and innovation must remain faithful to this architecture.

The Constitution governs everything. No role, no system, and no revenue opportunity stands above the Vision, the Graduate Profile, Formation, Discovery, Development, Deployment, or the Platform Constitution.

The Architecture protects the Constitution. Every dependency mapped in Section Ten, every boundary mapped in Section Six, and every ownership rule mapped in Section Nine exists so that growth never becomes an excuse for drift.

The Vision guides the Architecture. When a future Architect faces a decision this document does not anticipate, the test is not what is most efficient or most profitable. The test is the same one Blueprint 01 already gave: does this help discover, develop, deploy, and multiply the God-given genius within every child.

The learner remains at the center of the entire system.

__This is the Master Architecture Map. This is how the DGENIE Constitution holds together.__
