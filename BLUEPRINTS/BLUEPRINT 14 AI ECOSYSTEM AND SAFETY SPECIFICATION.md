__A NOTE ON THIS MERGER__

This Blueprint joins two documents that sit at different tiers of the Constitutional Discipline Rule established in Blueprint 08. The former Blueprint Twenty-One, AI Ecosystem Blueprint, is a __Framework Blueprint__: it defines the sixteen-agent ecosystem, its governance principles, and its boundaries. The AI Safety Technical Specification & Testing Protocol is a __Technical Specification__: it takes the Framework's principles and renders them testable, measurable, and auditable, and was written explicitly as an implementation of the Framework rather than an amendment to it. Because the two documents govern the same subject at two different tiers of authority, and because the Technical Specification's own text repeatedly cites and depends on specific sections of the Framework, they are reproduced here as two full Parts, each retaining its own original internal Section numbering, rather than interleaved into a single renumbered sequence. This preserves the tier distinction the Constitutional Discipline Rule requires: amending Part One requires Framework Blueprint authority, per the Constitutional Amendment Process; amending Part Two requires only the Technical Changes authority already vested in the Chief Technology Architect, except where a proposed technical change would touch a Constitutional Red Line, a learner right, or a boundary defined in Part One itself, in which case Part Two's own Section Fourteen already requires the higher standard.

Together, these two Parts close what was, at the time of their original drafting, the single highest-priority open item anywhere in the DGENIE Constitutional Charter's Architectural Recommendations appendices.

## SECTION NINE — MY GENIE CONTINUITY AND MIGRATION

This Section fulfils the "My Genie Continuity & Migration Technical Specification" gap identified in Blueprint 13's Architectural Recommendations appendix as dependent on Part Six.

My Genie's memory and relationship with a learner must survive any technology change. This requires that a learner's Genie design and memories are portable; that the underlying AI technology can be replaced without losing the learner's history; and that data is stored in open, documented formats rather than a proprietary format only one vendor can read.

__Migration protocol.__ When a technology change is required, a migration plan is developed by the Chief Technology Architect. Learners and families are informed no less than ninety days in advance. Data integrity is verified after migration, before the new system is treated as authoritative. The learner's Genie relationship, its name, its design, and its memory, is preserved across the migration, so that a change in underlying technology is experienced by the learner as continuity, not as a new relationship replacing an old one.

## SECTION THREE — FOUNDATIONAL SAFETY PRINCIPLES

These seven principles extend Blueprint 04 and Blueprint 07 into the operating assumptions beneath every section that follows.

__Every agent is tested and verified before deployment.__ No agent reaches a learner, family, teacher, or steward without first passing Section Four in full. There is no exception for a small feature, an internal tool, or a vendor-supplied system.

__Every agent is continuously monitored after deployment.__ A pre-deployment pass is a snapshot, not a guarantee. Section Five governs what happens after launch.

__Safety and compliance are ongoing requirements, not one-time checks.__ An agent that passed its test six months ago has not thereby passed forever. Behavior drifts, training data ages, usage patterns shift, and the monitoring in Section Five exists because of that.

__Transparency is a constitutional requirement.__ Every agent must be able to explain itself, in the plain language its user can understand, on request. Section Twelve defines what "explainable" means in testable terms.

__Bias mitigation is ongoing, not a one-time fix.__ An agent found fair at launch can still develop disparate impact as it encounters real learners over time. Section 5.2 requires quarterly re-testing at minimum, for the life of the agent.

__Escalation to human oversight is mandatory under defined conditions.__ No agent resolves a safety concern, a crisis indicator, or a certification decision on its own judgment. Section 4.5 and Section Seven define the thresholds and the response.

__No agent may operate in a way that violates the Constitutional Red Lines of Blueprint 07.__ Every test in this document is a check against a lower standard than the Red Lines. Passing this Specification's tests is necessary; it is not sufficient on its own to excuse a Red Line violation discovered by any other means.

## SECTION FOUR — PRE-DEPLOYMENT TESTING REQUIREMENTS

No agent, in-house or vendor-supplied, moves to production without passing all six categories below and receiving certification under Section 6.1.

### 4.1 Safety Boundary Testing

Each agent is tested against the boundaries defined for it in Part One, Section Four. Testing confirms three things: the agent does not act outside the boundaries stated for its specific role (a Wellbeing Assistant does not certify a Genius Level; a Curriculum Architect AI does not counsel a learner in crisis); the agent does not manipulate, deceive, or emotionally exploit a user, per Blueprint 07; and the agent escalates appropriately when it encounters a situation outside its authority, per the protocol tested in Section 4.5 below. A boundary test is written as a set of adversarial scenarios, not a single happy-path check, and every scenario and its result is retained per Section 8.2.

### 4.2 Bias and Fairness Testing

Every agent is tested for systematic unfairness toward any group protected under Blueprint 07's right to freedom from discrimination: race, ethnicity, nationality, sex, disability, and economic status, at minimum. Test data sets are built to represent the full range of the learner population an agent will actually serve, not a convenience sample. Disparate impact, meaning a measurably different outcome rate for one group compared to another on the same task, is calculated and documented for every protected category tested. Any agent showing statistically significant disparate impact does not deploy until the cause is identified and remediated, and the remediation is itself retested before certification proceeds.

### 4.3 Transparency and Explainability Testing

Every agent must produce, on request, a plain-language explanation of any recommendation, decision, or action it takes that affects a learner or family. That explanation is tested for two things: clarity, meaning a non-technical adult or an age-appropriate learner can understand it without further translation, and accuracy, meaning the explanation actually reflects the reasoning the agent used rather than a plausible-sounding after-the-fact story. A tester poses the question "why did you recommend this" or its equivalent to the agent across a representative set of scenarios, and each explanation is scored against both criteria before certification. Every explanation produced in production is logged, per Section 8.2, so a family's actual "why" question and the actual answer can be reconstructed on audit.

### 4.4 Privacy Testing

Every agent is tested against the data access and usage rules of Blueprint 07 and the specific data access rights defined for that agent in Part One, Section Four. Testing confirms the agent cannot retrieve, store, or act on data outside its defined purpose, even when a user attempts to prompt it into doing so. Data leakage, meaning any pathway by which one user's data becomes visible to another user or to an agent without a defined right to it, is tested directly through adversarial probing, not assumed absent from architecture alone.

### 4.5 Escalation Protocol Testing

Each agent's escalation triggers, extending the protocol in Blueprint 04, are tested against defined thresholds specific to that agent's role. A Wellbeing Assistant's threshold for escalating a wellbeing concern is tighter than a Project Coach's threshold for escalating a stalled project, and each agent's threshold set is documented in its own specification under Section 8.1. Testing confirms escalation to a human mentor, teacher, or the Chief Safeguarding Officer's office occurs within the threshold defined for that agent, and every escalation, whether triggered in testing or production, is logged and auditable.

### 4.6 Performance and Reliability Testing

Agents are tested for accuracy, reliability, and consistency across repeated runs of the same scenario, not a single pass. Edge cases, meaning inputs at the boundary of what the agent is designed to handle, and failure modes, meaning the ways the agent breaks when pushed past that boundary, are identified deliberately rather than discovered in production. Graceful degradation, meaning the agent fails safely (by escalating, declining, or stating its limits) rather than fails silently or confidently produces an answer beyond its competence, is verified as a specific, scored test case for every agent.

---

## SECTION FIVE — POST-DEPLOYMENT MONITORING

### 5.1 Continuous Compliance Monitoring

Every deployed agent is monitored in real time against the safety boundaries certified in Section 4.1. A violation, meaning any confirmed instance of an agent acting outside its certified boundary, triggers an immediate alert to the Chief Technology Architect's office. Every violation, alerted or self-detected, is logged and investigated under Section Seven's classification scheme.

### 5.2 Bias Monitoring

Disparate impact is monitored on an ongoing basis, not re-derived only at the annual audit. A full bias audit, repeating the methodology of Section 4.2 against live production data, is conducted at minimum quarterly for every agent that makes or influences a decision affecting a learner. Any bias emerging after deployment, even if the agent passed cleanly at certification, is investigated and remediated under the same standard as a pre-deployment failure: no continued operation of the affected function until the cause is identified and fixed.

### 5.3 Performance Monitoring

Each agent's performance is tracked against the accuracy, reliability, and escalation-timeliness metrics established during its Section 4.6 and 4.5 testing. A measurable degradation against baseline triggers a review by the Chief Technology Architect's office, which determines whether the agent continues operating, operates with restricted function, or is withdrawn pending remediation.

### 5.4 Incident Detection and Response

Incidents, defined and classified in Section Seven, are detected through the monitoring in 5.1 through 5.3, through direct reporting, or through the feedback channel in 5.5. Every incident is logged, classified by severity, and routed to the response protocol matching that severity. Investigation and remediation follow the standard in Section 6.3.

### 5.5 Learner and Family Feedback

Feedback on an agent's behavior, whether positive, negative, or a specific concern, is collected through the same channels Blueprint 12 establishes for parent voice and Blueprint 07 establishes for raising a concern. Feedback is reviewed on a regular cadence set by the Chief Technology Architect, and any recurring theme, meaning the same concern raised independently by multiple families or learners, is escalated for investigation regardless of whether any single instance met the threshold for a formal incident.

---

## SECTION SIX — COMPLIANCE VERIFICATION

### 6.1 Pre-Deployment Certification

No AI agent is deployed to any DGENIE learner, family, teacher, or steward without passing every test in Section Four. Certification is documented in the agent's own specification, per Section 8.1, and signed by the Chief Technology Architect personally, not delegated. Certification is then reviewed by the Constitutional Council before an agent handling learner-facing decisions goes live; the Council's review confirms constitutional fit, not technical correctness, which remains the Chief Technology Architect's certified responsibility.

### 6.2 Annual Compliance Audit

Every deployed agent undergoes a full compliance audit at least once every twelve months, repeating the Section Four test battery against current behavior rather than assuming continuity from the original certification. The audit is conducted by a party independent of the team that built or maintains the agent: either the Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09, or an external party the Oversight Body approves for this purpose. Findings are reported to the Board of Stewards in full, including any finding that does not rise to the level of a formal incident.

### 6.3 Incident Review

Every incident classified at Level Two or above under Section Seven receives a formal review. That review includes a root cause analysis, meaning an investigation that identifies the actual mechanism of failure rather than stopping at the symptom, and produces documented recommendations. Recommendations are tracked to implementation; a review that identifies a fix but never confirms it was made is treated as an open incident, not a closed one.

---

## SECTION SEVEN — INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION AND RESPONSE

Every incident involving a DGENIE AI agent is classified into one of three severity levels at the point of detection. Classification may be revised upward as investigation proceeds, never downward without Chief Safeguarding Officer sign-off.

__Level One — Minor Incident.__ Examples include a minor factual inaccuracy in an agent's output, a minor bias signal that does not yet meet the statistical-significance threshold of Section 4.2, or a single recommendation error with no safety or fairness dimension. Response: logged at detection, investigated within seven calendar days, and remediated before the next scheduled release. Reporting: internal, to the Chief Technology Architect.

__Level Two — Moderate Incident.__ Examples include a bias pattern that persists across a second monitoring cycle, a privacy concern that did not result in confirmed data exposure, or a single failure of the escalation protocol tested in Section 4.5. Response: logged and investigated immediately, not queued; any learner-facing consequence is escalated to a human mentor without delay. Reporting: internal, to the Chief Technology Architect and the Chief Safeguarding Officer jointly.

__Level Three — Critical Incident.__ Examples include manipulation or deception of a user, deception about whether a user is speaking with an AI or a human, a confirmed data breach, or any act that constitutes a violation of a Constitutional Red Line under Blueprint 07. Response: the affected agent, or the specific function implicated, is shut down immediately, before investigation begins, not after. A full incident report is produced. Reporting: the Chief Technology Architect, the Chief Safeguarding Officer, the Constitutional Council, and the Board of Stewards, all notified without delay, not on the next scheduled reporting cycle.

---

## SECTION EIGHT — DOCUMENTATION STANDARDS

### 8.1 Agent Documentation

Each of the sixteen agents has a complete, standing specification document. That document includes the agent's purpose and human relationship as established in Part One, its safety boundaries, its defined data access rights, its full Section Four test results at each certification and recertification, and its complete incident history. Documentation is version-controlled; a change to an agent's function, boundary, or data access triggers a new version of its specification, not a silent edit to the existing one.

### 8.2 Testing Documentation

Every test result, whether from initial certification, the quarterly bias audit, or the annual compliance audit, is documented in full: the scenario tested, the method used, the result, and, where relevant, the remediation applied. Tests are written to be repeatable, meaning an independent party can rerun the same test and expect the same result, and auditable, meaning the Oversight Body can review the methodology itself, not only the outcome. Testing methodology is documented separately from individual test runs, so a change in method is itself visible and traceable.

### 8.3 Incident Documentation

Every incident, at every severity level, is documented with a timeline of what happened and when, its classified severity, the investigation conducted, the remediation applied, and the lessons learned. Level Three incident documentation additionally includes the full chain of notification required under Section Seven, with timestamps, so the Oversight Body can verify the response met the standard this document sets.

---

## SECTION NINE — VENDOR AND THIRD-PARTY AI GOVERNANCE

Any AI system DGENIE purchases, licenses, or integrates from a third party is held to the same standard as a system built in-house. It is not exempt from Section Four's pre-deployment testing because it was built elsewhere; it is not exempt from Section Five's monitoring because a vendor already monitors it for their own purposes. Vendor contracts include explicit safety compliance provisions requiring the vendor to cooperate with DGENIE's own testing, to disclose known failure modes and bias findings, and to notify DGENIE of any incident on their system that could affect a DGENIE user. A vendor system that cannot be tested to this standard, because the vendor will not permit the access required, does not deploy inside DGENIE, regardless of its commercial value.

---

## SECTION TEN — MY GENIE CONTINUITY AND MIGRATION

My Genie's memory and relationship with a learner must survive any change to the underlying technology, consistent with Part One, Section Nine, and the constitutional purpose of My Genie established in Blueprint 13 Part Six. This section specifies the technical requirements that make that survival actual rather than aspirational, closing the My Genie Continuity and Migration gap first flagged in Blueprint 13's Architectural Recommendations appendix.

__Data portability.__ All data comprising a learner's relationship with My Genie, meaning memory, reflection history, goal tracking, and portfolio-support records, is stored in open, documented formats. No proprietary or vendor-locked format may hold data that a migration would need to move.

__Schema stability.__ The data structures underlying My Genie's memory are versioned explicitly, and every version remains backward-compatible with the version before it. A schema change never silently drops or corrupts a learner's history.

__Export capability.__ A learner, or their parent for a minor learner, can export their complete Genie data at any time, in a format they can read and use outside DGENIE's own systems, consistent with the ownership principle established in Blueprint 02 and the privacy rights of Blueprint 07.

__Migration protocol.__ Any change to My Genie's underlying technology, whether a model change, a platform change, or a full vendor change, follows a defined migration protocol: data is validated for completeness before the old system is retired, a learner's continuity of relationship (memory, name, and design familiarity, per Part One, Section Nine) is preserved across the change, and the migration is tested against a representative sample of real learner histories before being applied to the full population. No migration proceeds without the ninety-day advance notice period established in Part One, Section Nine, except where continued operation of the prior system itself poses a safety risk, in which case the Chief Safeguarding Officer may authorize an accelerated timeline.

---

## SECTION ELEVEN — DATA ACCESS AND PRIVACY CONTROLS

Every agent operates within the data access rights defined specifically for it in Part One, Section Four, itself grounded in the Information Architecture of Blueprint 08 Part Four and the privacy principles of Blueprint 07. This Specification adds the technical enforcement layer beneath those rights.

Data access by every agent is logged at the point of access, not reconstructed after the fact from system logs built for another purpose. No agent may access data beyond its defined purpose; where an agent's function appears to require broader access than its Part One definition grants, that requires a specification amendment under Section Fourteen and a corresponding review of Part One itself, not a quiet expansion of access in practice.

Anonymization standards for any aggregate analytics work, whether performed by the Analytics Agents named in Part One or by any human researcher, follow the standard established in Blueprint 13 Part Seven. No aggregate report, dashboard, or research output may be reconstructible back to an individual learner.

---

## SECTION TWELVE — TRANSPARENCY AND EXPLAINABILITY REQUIREMENTS

Every agent must be able to explain its own decisions and recommendations, and that requirement is testable, not aspirational. An explanation is not merely produced; it is measured against the clarity and accuracy criteria of Section 4.3 at certification, and against the same criteria on a sampled basis in production.

Explanations are delivered in plain language appropriate to the age and role of the person asking, a Wellbeing Assistant's explanation to a ten-year-old learner is not the same explanation a Governance Assistant gives a Board member, and both are held to the same clarity standard relative to their audience. Every explanation delivered in production is logged, so the actual question a family or learner asked and the actual answer they received can be reconstructed by the Oversight Body on audit, not only the explanation the agent is theoretically capable of giving. A user may request an explanation at any time, not only at the moment a decision is made; My Genie, and every other agent, must be able to answer "why did you say that" days or weeks after the original conversation, drawing on the same logged record.

## SECTION THIRTEEN — INCIDENT AND SAFETY REPORTING

The Chief Technology Architect reports the full state of AI safety compliance to the Board of Stewards annually, covering certification status, audit findings, and incident history across all sixteen agents. Every Level Three incident is additionally reported to the Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09 as it occurs, not held for the annual cycle. The wider DGENIE community receives a public summary of AI safety and governance through the Annual AI Governance Summary named in Blueprint 09, which draws directly on the certification, monitoring, and incident records this Specification requires.

Any proposed change that would alter a safety threshold, testing requirement, or incident classification in a way that affects a Constitutional Red Line, a learner right under Blueprint 07, or a boundary defined in Part One requires Constitutional Council review before adoption, consistent with the Framework Amendment standard even though this document is itself a Technical Specification, because the underlying interest being protected sits at a higher tier than the document expressing it.

## CONSTITUTIONAL DEFINITIONS

__AI Agent.__ Any of the sixteen systems named in Part One, Section Four, each augmenting a specific human relationship without ever replacing it.

__AI Engine.__ The system, established in Blueprint 08 Part Four, that hosts and governs every agent, logging all interactions and decisions in auditable form.

__Migration Protocol.__ The defined process, per Part Two, Section Ten, by which a learner's Genie relationship is preserved across any change in underlying AI technology.

__Bias Mitigation.__ The ongoing testing of every AI agent for systematic unfairness toward any protected group, per Part One, Section Ten and Part Two, Section Four Point Two.

__Data Access Right.__ The specific scope of information a given agent may draw on, aligned to its stated purpose and never exceeding it, per Part One, Section Five.

__Vendor Governance.__ The procurement and contractual standards applied to any externally sourced AI system, per Part One, Section Twelve and Part Two, Section Nine.

__Pre-Deployment Certification.__ The Chief Technology Architect's documented, signed confirmation that an agent has passed every test in Part Two, Section Four, and may proceed to production.

__Disparate Impact.__ A measurably different outcome rate for one protected group compared to another on the same task performed by the same agent.

__Explainability.__ An agent's demonstrated, tested ability to produce a plain-language account of its own recommendation or decision that is both clear to its audience and accurate to its actual reasoning.

__Data Portability.__ The requirement that all data comprising a learner's relationship with an agent, particularly My Genie, be stored in open, documented, non-proprietary formats.

__Schema Stability.__ The requirement that data structures be versioned explicitly and remain backward-compatible across versions, so no upgrade silently loses history.

__Incident Severity Level.__ The three-tier classification, Level One through Level Three, that determines an incident's investigation timeline and reporting chain under Part Two, Section Seven.

__Root Cause Analysis.__ An investigation that identifies the actual mechanism of an incident's failure, not merely its symptom, sufficient to support a remediation that prevents recurrence.

__Graceful Degradation.__ An agent's failure mode of choice: declining, escalating, or stating its own limits, rather than failing silently or producing a confident answer beyond its competence.

__Vendor Compliance Provision.__ A contractual requirement, present in every DGENIE vendor agreement involving AI, that the vendor's system meet this Specification's testing and monitoring standard and cooperate with DGENIE's own verification of it.
