These four documents were always one conversation happening in four separate rooms. Stewardship named the philosophy: every resource DGENIE holds, money included, is entrusted rather than owned. # PART ONE — THE STEWARDSHIP & SUSTAINABILITY CONSTITUTION

__PRIMARY OBJECTIVE__

This is not primarily a finance document. It is the moral, institutional, and strategic philosophy governing how DGENIE receives, uses, protects, and multiplies every resource entrusted to it, judged by long-term mission faithfulness rather than organizational survival alone.

__THE FOUNDATIONAL CONSTITUTIONAL DECLARATION__

__Every resource entrusted to DGENIE ultimately belongs to God and is held in trust for the flourishing of learners, families, communities and future generations. Therefore every decision concerning people, money, knowledge, technology, time, relationships and influence shall be governed by faithful stewardship rather than ownership or exploitation.__

Every part of this Part works out this one sentence.

## SECTION ONE — THE PHILOSOPHY OF STEWARDSHIP

__Ownership__ claims the ultimate right to dispose of something as one pleases. __Stewardship__ manages what belongs to another, for that owner's intended purpose, and answers to the owner for how it was used. __Possession__ is the plain fact of holding or using something, which may or may not carry any claim of right over it. __Responsibility__ is the duty to answer for how an entrusted thing was used. __Faithfulness__ is consistent right action over time, whether or not anyone is watching. __Accountability__ is the actual, structured practice of being called to answer, distinct from the private disposition of faithfulness. __Legacy__ is what remains of a steward's use of a trust once the steward is gone.

DGENIE holds every resource named in Section Two as a steward, not an owner. This distinction is not a sentiment. It is a governing constraint on every decision the institution makes.

__Why DGENIE rejects institutional self-preservation in favor of mission stewardship__

An institution that measures its own success by its continued existence will, eventually, make decisions that protect the institution at the expense of the people it exists to serve. History supplies this pattern often enough that DGENIE names it in advance and refuses it by constitution rather than by good intention alone. A steward is judged by faithfulness to the purpose entrusted to them, not by whether the institution they served merely continued. Where a choice must be made between DGENIE's institutional continuation and its founding mission, this Constitution requires the mission to win, even at cost to the institution.

## SECTION TWO — THE RESOURCES ENTRUSTED TO DGENIE

__Learners__ are the reason DGENIE exists and must never be treated as a means to any institutional end.

__Families__ entrust their children and their trust to DGENIE, and that trust, once broken, is not easily rebuilt.

__Human relationships__, every mentor, teacher, and community bond, took years to build and can be lost quickly through carelessness.

__Time__ is unrecoverable; a learner's childhood, once spent, is spent, and every institutional decision either honors or wastes it.

__Knowledge__ accumulated across the Charter must serve future learners, not sit unused or be hoarded.

__Educational content__ built for one learner's growth belongs, in principle, to the mission that produced it, not to any single office.

__Research__ conducted in DGENIE's name carries a responsibility to the wider field of education, not merely to DGENIE's own advantage.

__Curriculum__, the product of Blueprint 04, represents years of collective design and must be protected from casual or unexamined change.

__Technology__ is a tool entrusted for service, detailed fully in Section Six.

__Artificial Intelligence__ carries the specific stewardship obligations already established in Blueprint 13 and restated here as a resource category.

__Community trust__, once damaged, is the hardest resource on this list to restore, and every decision should weigh this cost explicitly.

__Reputation__ is the outward face of community trust and must never be protected through concealment, per Blueprint 07's commitment to truthfulness.

__Financial resources__ entrusted by families, donors, and partners fund the mission and belong to that mission, not to any office's discretion.

__Facilities__, present and future, physical or otherwise, exist to serve learning, not to signal institutional stature.

__Digital infrastructure__ carries the learner's data and daily experience and must be maintained with the seriousness that responsibility deserves.

__Data__ about a learner belongs, per Blueprint 07, to that learner and their family, held by DGENIE only in trust.

__Institutional memory__, established in Blueprint 13, preserves the reasoning behind DGENIE's decisions for those who come after.

__Partnerships__ extend DGENIE's mission and must never be allowed to compromise it, per Section Seven.

__Volunteer service__ is freely given and must never be treated as an entitlement or taken for granted.

__Creative works__ produced by learners, mentors, and staff carry the dignity of their creators and must be handled with the same care.

__Masterpieces__, the culminating projects of Blueprint 05, represent a learner's own life work and remain theirs, per Section Five.

__Intellectual property__ generated across the Charter must advance the mission, not become a private asset pursued for its own sake.

__Cultural heritage__, the traditions and identity of the communities DGENIE serves, must be honored rather than displaced by a uniform institutional culture.

__Prayer and spiritual support__ sustain the whole community and are treated here as a genuine resource, not a decorative afterthought.

Each of these must be stewarded wisely because DGENIE holds none of them by right. Every one was entrusted, and every one can be misused, wasted, or exploited if the institution ever forgets that the entrusting itself is the deeper fact.

## SECTION THREE — THE STEWARDSHIP OF LEARNERS

Learners are never institutional assets. They are persons entrusted to the care of the community, and this Section restates, in stewardship terms, the dignity already established as a constitutional right in Blueprint 07.

DGENIE shall never exploit a learner, treating their needs as a means to institutional gain. DGENIE shall never commercialize a learner, turning their story, image, or success into a product without their and their family's free and informed consent. DGENIE shall never manipulate a learner, using persuasive design or emotional pressure to serve any purpose other than their own growth, consistent with the AI Safety principles in Blueprint 07. DGENIE shall never treat a learner's success as institutional property, claiming credit that belongs, first, to the learner, their family, and their mentors.

Learner flourishing always comes before institutional advantage. Where the two conflict, and they sometimes will, this Constitution requires flourishing to prevail.

## SECTION FOUR — FINANCIAL STEWARDSHIP (PRINCIPLES)

Financial resources exist to fund the mission, not to become an end pursued for their own sake. This Section states the governing principles; Part Two below gives them full operational form.

__Mission before profit.__ Every financial decision is tested first against whether it serves the Vision established in Blueprint 01, not against whether it maximizes revenue or surplus.

__Financial transparency.__ Families and the community are entitled to a plain account of how resources entrusted to DGENIE are used, consistent with the Transparency principle in Blueprint 08.

__Long-term sustainability.__ DGENIE plans for decades, not fiscal quarters, and resists decisions that improve a single year's position at the cost of the mission's endurance.

__Responsible growth.__ Growth is pursued only at a pace the institution's people, systems, and character can actually sustain, never for its own sake.

__Generosity.__ DGENIE gives, and teaches its learners to give, treating financial capacity as an occasion for generosity rather than accumulation.

__Scholarships.__ Financial capacity should not be the barrier that determines who receives a DGENIE education, and scholarship provision is a constitutional expectation, not a discretionary gesture.

__Accessibility.__ Pricing structures are designed with the widest reasonable reach in mind, consistent with the Two-Track Delivery Model's differing costs of service.

__Fair pricing.__ Prices reflect the actual cost of faithful stewardship, not what the market alone would bear.

__Responsible reserves.__ DGENIE maintains reserves sufficient to weather disruption without compromising its mission under pressure, but never accumulates reserves as an end in themselves.

__Ethical fundraising.__ Funds are raised honestly, without manipulation of donors or misrepresentation of need, and every gift is used for the purpose it was given.

__Avoiding compromising dependence.__ DGENIE guards against financial dependence on any single funder, partner, or revenue source whose influence could compel a decision inconsistent with this Charter.

## SECTION FIVE — KNOWLEDGE & INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY STEWARDSHIP

__Curriculum,__ built through the process in Blueprint 04, is protected from casual alteration but never locked away from the learners it exists to serve.

__Research__ conducted under DGENIE's name is shared honestly with the wider field where doing so serves the mission of education generally, not withheld merely to protect competitive advantage.

__Creative works and Masterpieces__ belong, first, to the learners who made them; DGENIE's role is custodial, per Blueprint 13, holding a copy in institutional memory only with the learner's consent.

__Educational resources__ developed at DGENIE's expense are treated as mission assets, to be reused and improved rather than duplicated wastefully.

__Software__ built for DGENIE serves the mission and is licensed and protected only to the extent necessary to sustain that service, not to maximize its market value.

__AI systems__ built or licensed for DGENIE remain bound by the constitutional purpose established in Blueprint 13: to support, never to dominate.

__Institutional knowledge,__ the accumulated reasoning of this Charter itself, is treated as a resource to be protected from loss and made available to those who steward DGENIE after the current generation.

__Balancing openness and protection.__ DGENIE shares generously where sharing serves the mission and protects only where protection is necessary to sustain the mission's continuation, never protection for its own sake and never openness that would compromise a learner's rights under Blueprint 07.

## SECTION SIX — TECHNOLOGY STEWARDSHIP

Cross-reference Blueprint 13 in full: technology is entrusted to DGENIE for service, never for dominance, and this Section extends that principle from the philosophy of intelligence into the practice of stewardship.

__Responsible innovation.__ New technology is adopted because it serves learners better, not because it is new.

__Security.__ Every system entrusted with a learner's data is protected with the seriousness Blueprint 07's Privacy article requires.

__Maintainability.__ DGENIE avoids building or adopting systems too complex or too poorly documented for a future steward to responsibly maintain.

__Interoperability.__ Systems are built, where practical, to exchange data and integrate with others, avoiding the isolation that traps institutional knowledge inside a single tool.

__Technology neutrality.__ This Charter commits to no single vendor, platform, or technical architecture; the mission does not change when the tools serving it do.

__Long-term preservation.__ Records, portfolios, and institutional memory are kept in formats and systems built to outlast any single technology's popularity.

__Ethical procurement.__ Technology is purchased from providers whose practices do not contradict this Charter's commitments to learner dignity and data stewardship.

__Vendor independence, where practical.__ DGENIE resists building critical dependence on a single provider whose future decisions could compromise the mission's continuation.

## SECTION SEVEN — PARTNERSHIP STEWARDSHIP (PRINCIPLES)

DGENIE partners with families, churches, schools, universities, governments, businesses, non-profit organizations, technology providers, researchers, and international partners, each relationship entered for the sake of the mission, never for institutional prestige alone. This Section states the governing principles; Part Three below gives them a full evaluation process.

No partnership may compromise constitutional integrity. Where a partner's terms, expectations, or influence would require DGENIE to violate any article of this Charter, the partnership is declined or ended, regardless of the resources or opportunity it offers.

Mission precedes opportunity. An opportunity that would advance DGENIE's reach or resources at the cost of its founding purpose is not, by this Constitution's definition, an opportunity at all.

## SECTION EIGHT — ENVIRONMENTAL & COMMUNITY STEWARDSHIP

DGENIE is digital-first, per Blueprint 06, but this does not exempt it from responsibility toward the communities and the creation it exists within.

__Responsible resource use__ governs every facility, device, and system DGENIE operates, avoiding waste as a matter of principle, not merely cost.

__Environmental awareness__ shapes decisions about infrastructure, including the energy and material footprint of the digital systems the Digital-First Learning Community depends on.

__Community investment__ means DGENIE gives back to the local communities its Local Learning Circles inhabit, not merely draws from them.

__Local impact__ is measured honestly: does a given community's DGENIE presence leave it stronger, per the Contribution Principle established in Blueprint 05.

__Service__ to the surrounding community is treated as integral to DGENIE's identity, not a separate charitable program.

__Long-term thinking__ governs every environmental and community decision, weighing a choice's effect decades ahead, not only its immediate cost.

__Future campuses,__ should DGENIE ever establish physical sites, are planned with the same stewardship standard applied to every digital resource.

__Digital sustainability__ treats server capacity, energy use, and device lifecycle as legitimate stewardship concerns, not merely technical details left to a vendor's discretion.

## SECTION NINE — INNOVATION STEWARDSHIP

DGENIE encourages innovation without abandoning constitutional identity. New technologies, AI advances, educational models, research discoveries, and institutional experiments are all welcomed as potential gifts to the mission, and all are evaluated the same way: does this innovation serve the Vision in Blueprint 01, strengthen the Formation Framework in Blueprint 03, and respect the boundaries already set in Blueprints 07, 08, and 13.

Every innovation must strengthen, never replace, the Constitution. An innovation that would require rewriting a Constitutional Blueprint to accommodate it is not, by definition, a small adjustment; it is reviewed through the Expansion Governance process established in Blueprint 08 and Part Four below, not adopted quietly through operational decision alone.

## SECTION TEN — GENERATIONAL SUSTAINABILITY

DGENIE serves not only the learners enrolled today but the generations who have not yet arrived.

__Institutional continuity__ requires that no single office, leader, or technology be so central to DGENIE's operation that its loss would threaten the mission's survival.

__Leadership development__ extends the succession planning required in Blueprint 08, treating the deliberate formation of future stewards as a present obligation, not a future problem.

__Knowledge preservation__ relies on the Institutional Memory architecture in Blueprint 13, ensuring that what one generation of stewards learned is not lost to the next.

__Financial resilience,__ per Section Four and Part Two below, ensures the mission can weather disruption without compromising its founding commitments under pressure.

__Mission inheritance__ means every new steward, mentor, or learner who joins DGENIE receives, deliberately, the reasoning behind this Charter, not merely its rules.

__Succession__ follows the process established in Blueprint 08, applied here specifically to the stewardship of resources: no resource category in Section Two is left without a clear line of responsible transfer.

__Long-term planning__ treats a century, not a decade, as the relevant unit of institutional thinking, consistent with this Charter's stated hundred-year horizon.

__Constitutional fidelity__ across generations is the ultimate test of every principle in this Part: a DGENIE two generations removed from its founders should still be recognizably faithful to Blueprint 01's Vision.

## SECTION ELEVEN — THE ECONOMICS OF THE KINGDOM

Economic activity within DGENIE exists to enable human flourishing, not to maximize institutional wealth. This Section names the moral economy underlying every principle in this Part.

__Generosity__ is the default posture of a steward who understands that what they hold was given, not earned outright; DGENIE gives before it is asked, where it is able.

__Justice__ requires that DGENIE's economic decisions never extract more from a family, partner, or community than fairness allows, and never withhold from a learner what their flourishing requires because of their family's financial circumstance.

__Service__ is the purpose economic activity exists to fund; revenue is a means, and the moment it becomes an end, this Constitution has been violated in spirit even where no specific rule has been broken.

__Opportunity__ is what DGENIE's economic stewardship exists to create for learners and families who would otherwise lack it, per the Scholarships and Accessibility principles in Section Four.

__Dignity__ governs how DGENIE treats every person touched by its economic decisions, staff, families, and partners alike, refusing to reduce any of them to a line item.

__Stewardship__ is named again here deliberately: every principle in this Section is an economic expression of the philosophy established in Section One.

__Faithfulness__ measures whether DGENIE's economic choices, examined years later, still serve the purpose they were made for.

__Community__ is both the beneficiary and the participant in DGENIE's economic life; resources move through the community, strengthening it, rather than being extracted from it toward a distant institutional center.

This is the moral economy DGENIE commits to: an economy that measures its success by the flourishing it produces, not the wealth it accumulates.

## SECTION TWELVE — THE CONSTITUTIONAL STEWARDSHIP COVENANT

Every steward of DGENIE, in every constitutional office established across this Charter, shall use every entrusted resource with humility, wisdom, transparency, and faithfulness, for the benefit of learners, families, communities, and future generations.

The mission is always greater than the institution.

The learner is always more valuable than the platform.

Faithfulness is valued above growth.

Stewardship is valued above ownership.

Legacy is measured by transformed lives rather than accumulated assets.

__This is the DGENIE Stewardship & Sustainability Constitution. Every resource entrusted to this institution is held in trust for the flourishing it was given to serve, and for nothing less.__

# PART TWO — FINANCIAL STEWARDSHIP & RESOURCE GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

## SECTION ONE — PURPOSE & SCOPE

This Part governs all financial resources entrusted to DGENIE, per the resource category established in Part One, Section Two. It applies to every office, role, and entity operating under DGENIE's constitutional authority, including the Board of Stewards, the Chief Executive, every Chief Officer, all staff with financial authority, and all volunteers handling funds.

This Part does not replace jurisdictional compliance. Tax filings, employment law, charitable registration, and every other legal or regulatory obligation in the jurisdictions DGENIE operates within remain an operational responsibility, discharged in a manner consistent with, but not defined by, this document.

## SECTION TWO — FOUNDATIONAL FINANCIAL PRINCIPLES

This Section extends, without redefining, the principles established in Part One, Section Four: mission before profit, financial transparency, long-term sustainability measured in decades rather than fiscal quarters, responsible growth paced to what the institution can actually sustain, generosity as the default posture, scholarships as a constitutional expectation rather than a discretionary gesture, fair pricing reflecting the actual cost of faithful stewardship, responsible reserves, ethical fundraising, and avoidance of financial dependence that could compromise mission integrity.

## SECTION THREE — BUDGETING FRAMEWORK

__The annual budget cycle__ runs in three phases: preparation, beginning no later than four months before the close of the fiscal year; committee review, occupying the following six weeks; and Board approval, completed no later than thirty days before the new fiscal year begins.

__Preparation__ is the Chief Executive's responsibility, conducted under the oversight of the Finance & Audit Committee established in Blueprint 08, Part Two, which reviews the draft budget for alignment with this Framework before it reaches the full Board.

__Approval__ rests with the Board of Stewards, exercised as a Major Decision under Blueprint 08, Part Two, requiring the supermajority threshold defined there.

__Budget categories__ include, at minimum, personnel, educational programs, technology and platform infrastructure, reserves, scholarships, community investment, and administration. Each category is reported separately in every budget document.

__Variance reporting__ occurs quarterly, and any category tracking more than ten percent above or below its budgeted figure at the midpoint of the fiscal year triggers a specific explanation to the Finance & Audit Committee.

__Budgeting method.__ DGENIE adopts a hybrid model: incremental budgeting in ordinary years, combined with a full zero-based review of every program category once every three fiscal years.

## SECTION FOUR — RESERVE POLICY

DGENIE maintains an operating reserve of no less than six months of operating expenses, with a target range of six to twelve months, the specific target within that range set annually by the Board as part of budget adoption.

__Purpose.__ Reserves exist to protect mission continuity through disruption, to fund emergency response, and to allow DGENIE to act on a genuine strategic opportunity without compromising ordinary operations, never to accumulate as an end in themselves.

__Drawdown.__ A reserve drawdown below the six-month floor requires a supermajority Board vote and a written declaration of the emergency or opportunity justifying it.

__Replenishment.__ Any drawdown is accompanied by a replenishment plan, approved at the same meeting as the drawdown itself.

## SECTION FIVE — PRICING & ACCESSIBILITY MODEL

__Fee structure.__ Home Track and Membership Track carry differing fee structures, reflecting their differing costs of service established in Blueprint 04, and each is reviewed annually against the Fair Pricing principle in Part One, Section Four.

__An unmodeled assumption, named directly, added 2026-08-15.__ Blueprint 04 Part Five states that a Mentor's caseload is one of the reasons the Membership Track's fortnightly cadence is priced and paced as it is; this Blueprint's own Fair Pricing principle and the Scholarship floor above both depend, without saying so, on a Mentor's time being a sustainable cost input at whatever caseload the institution actually runs. No document in this Charter has yet modeled those together, what a caseload ceiling would do to per-family cost, or what a family-affordable price does to sustainable caseload, in the other direction. This is not resolved here; it is named so it cannot be resolved by two separate future decisions, a caseload ceiling set in Blueprint 15's evidence architecture and a pricing figure set in this Section, that never check against each other. Any future caseload ceiling and any future revision to the fee structure above are required to be modeled against each other and against the Scholarship floor before either is finalized, not set independently.

__Mentor compensation model, added 2026-08-15, closing part of the unmodeled assumption above on Duncan's direction.__ On the Membership Track, a Mentor receives twenty percent of that learner's subscription fee, and ten percent of the same fee funds the scholarship pool named below, both calculated per learner at the point the fee is collected rather than only reconciled against net revenue at fiscal year-end. This ten percent is the same constitutional floor stated in the Scholarship provision immediately below, expressed as a direct, per-transaction earmark rather than a separate obligation stacked on top of it; a future revision should confirm this reading explicitly if a different relationship was intended. On the Home Track, a Mentor is paid the same flat amount per learner they would receive for a Membership Track learner, not twenty percent of the Home Track fee, which is priced higher to reflect its daily cadence and its Teacher role rather than to proportionally increase Mentor compensation. This defines a compensation rate, not by itself a caseload ceiling; it tells a Mentor what each assigned learner is worth, not how many a Mentor may hold at once. __Caseload ceiling set, added 2026-08-15, later the same day.__ Blueprint 11 Section Four now sets that number: twenty learners per Mentor, combined across both tracks, adopted as a starting figure on Duncan's direction rather than one derived from caseload-cost evidence. The modeling check this Section's own paragraph above requires, that any caseload ceiling be checked against this compensation rate and the scholarship floor before being treated as final, has not yet been run; at twenty learners and a twenty-percent Membership Track rate, a Mentor's annual income from that track alone equals four times the Membership Track subscription price, a relationship now directly computable once an actual price is set, and the one remaining input this whole model is waiting on.

__Scholarship provision.__ DGENIE allocates a minimum of ten percent of net revenue to scholarships in every fiscal year, a constitutional floor rather than a target. The Board may set the actual figure higher through the ordinary budget process, but may not set it lower without a Framework amendment under Section Twelve below.

__Scholarship criteria__ combine need, assessed through a transparent, documented process, with a smaller merit-based allocation recognizing exceptional circumstance.

__Waiver process.__ A family unable to meet even a scholarship-adjusted fee may apply for a waiver, reviewed by the Chief Executive with Finance & Audit Committee visibility, and no learner is excluded from DGENIE solely because of inability to pay.

__Sliding scale.__ Where regional economic conditions vary significantly across DGENIE's Local Learning Circles, a sliding scale fee model may be applied.

## SECTION SIX — FINANCIAL CONTROLS & SEPARATION OF DUTIES

__Segregation of duties.__ No single person may initiate, approve, and reconcile the same transaction.

__Approval authority__ operates in three tiers, scaled to transaction size: a small-purchase tier, approvable by a single authorized staff member; a mid-range tier, requiring Chief Executive approval; and a major tier, requiring Finance & Audit Committee approval before the Chief Executive may act.

__Procurement and contracting__ above the mid-range tier require at least two comparative quotes or a documented justification for single-source procurement.

__Expense reimbursement__ requires original documentation and is approved by someone other than the person claiming reimbursement.

__Reconciliation__ of every bank and financial account occurs monthly, performed by someone with no authority to initiate transactions on that account.

__Signatory authority.__ Every bank account requires two authorized signatures for any transaction above the small-purchase tier; no single officer holds sole signatory authority over institutional funds.

## SECTION SEVEN — FINANCIAL AUDIT PROCEDURES

__Annual external audit.__ DGENIE commissions an independent, qualified accounting firm to conduct a full external audit every fiscal year, selected by the Finance & Audit Committee and rotated or re-evaluated no less than every five years.

__Scope.__ The audit examines all financial statements, internal controls, compliance with this Framework, and the use of restricted funds against donor intent.

__Timeline.__ The audit is completed and delivered within one hundred twenty days of fiscal year-end.

__Presentation.__ Audit results are presented first to the Finance & Audit Committee, then to the full Board.

__Management response.__ The Chief Executive delivers a written response to every audit finding within thirty days.

__Follow-up.__ Any material finding receives a dedicated follow-up review within six months.

## SECTION EIGHT — FINANCIAL REPORTING & TRANSPARENCY

DGENIE provides a __quarterly financial summary__ to the Board and an __annual financial report__ to the community, written in plain language accessible to a family without financial training, satisfying the public disclosure obligation in Blueprint 09.

__No hidden funds.__ DGENIE maintains no off-budget accounts and no undisclosed reserves.

## SECTION NINE — ETHICAL FUNDRAISING & DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES

Funds are raised honestly, without manipulation of a donor's emotion or circumstance. __Donor intent is honoured__ exactly as given; a gift made for a stated purpose is tracked as a restricted fund and used only for that purpose.

DGENIE does not fundraise from learners or families in any way that exploits the trust of the relationship established in Blueprint 07. No donor may dictate curriculum, formation, or constitutional policy; a gift conditioned on such influence is declined, regardless of its size.

## SECTION TEN — CONFLICT-OF-INTEREST DISCLOSURE PROCESS

__Who must disclose.__ Every Board member, every Chief Officer, all staff holding financial authority, and every volunteer who handles funds.

__What must be disclosed.__ Any financial interest in a vendor, partner, or competitor; any personal relationship reasonably capable of influencing a financial decision; and any gift or benefit received above a de minimis threshold set by Board resolution.

__When.__ Annually, and immediately upon any material change in circumstance.

__To whom.__ Disclosures are submitted to the Board's Governance & Nominating Committee, with copies provided to the Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09.

__Review.__ A flagged conflict is referred to the Board Chair, and, where any safeguarding implication exists, to the Chief Safeguarding Officer.

__Consequences of nondisclosure.__ Removal from the Board; termination of employment, where applicable; and referral to the Oversight Body for further review.

## SECTION ELEVEN — PARTNERSHIPS & RESOURCE STEWARDSHIP

Every partnership's financial terms are reviewed by the Finance & Audit Committee before the Board's approval. Partner contributions are tracked as restricted or unrestricted funds according to donor intent. No partnership may create a financial dependency that compromises DGENIE's constitutional independence; the Finance & Audit Committee monitors the proportion of total revenue drawn from any single partner and flags any relationship approaching a concerning concentration to the full Board.

Detailed due diligence standards for evaluating a prospective partner before this financial review even begins are given in full in Part Three below.

## SECTION TWELVE — FINANCIAL RISK MANAGEMENT

DGENIE identifies and monitors financial risk across four categories: __fraud__, mitigated through the segregation of duties and reconciliation discipline in Section Six; __revenue disruption__, mitigated through the reserve policy in Section Four and the scholarship floor's independence from any single revenue source; __currency fluctuation__, mitigated through the Finance & Audit Committee's regular review of exposure; and __partnership concentration__, mitigated through the monitoring described in Section Eleven.

__Business continuity financial planning__ ensures the reserve policy in Section Four is sufficient to sustain core operations through a defined disruption scenario.

__Insurance.__ DGENIE maintains insurance coverage appropriate to its operations, including general liability and directors' and officers' coverage.

## SECTION THIRTEEN — AMENDMENT OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Amendments to this Part require a proposal from the Chief Executive, review by the Finance & Audit Committee, and approval by the Board of Stewards, applying the Framework Amendments tier of the Amendment Process established in Blueprint 08. No amendment may reduce the scholarship floor in Section Five or the reserve floor in Section Four below the levels stated here without also amending Part One of this document.

# PART THREE — PARTNERSHIP EVALUATION & DUE DILIGENCE FRAMEWORK

## SECTION ONE — PURPOSE & SCOPE

This Part governs every partnership DGENIE enters, including relationships with families in their capacity as partners in formation; churches and faith organizations; schools, including partner schools; universities; governments at every level; businesses and corporations; non-profit organizations; technology providers; researchers and research institutions; and any other partner arrangement contemplated by Part One, Section Seven.

## SECTION TWO — FOUNDATIONAL PARTNERSHIP PRINCIPLES

__Mission first.__ Every partnership serves the Vision established in Blueprint 01; a partnership that does not is not evaluated further, regardless of the resources it offers.

__Constitutional integrity.__ No partnership may require DGENIE to violate any article of this Charter.

__Learner protection.__ Every partnership is evaluated for its impact on learner safety and dignity, integrated into due diligence per Section Four rather than treated as a separate check performed afterward.

__Transparency.__ Partnerships are disclosed to the community with detail appropriate to their significance, per Section Ten.

__No compromising dependence.__ DGENIE avoids partnerships that create dependency on a single partner, extending the Financial Dependency principle in Part Two, Section Eleven to every form of partnership, not only financial ones.

__Mutual benefit.__ A partnership should strengthen both parties.

__Accountability.__ Every partnership is monitored and reviewed regularly, per Section Seven.

__Termination.__ A partnership that no longer serves the mission, or that comes to compromise constitutional integrity, is ended, per Section Eight, regardless of its history or convenience.

## SECTION THREE — PARTNERSHIP TYPOLOGY

__Tier 1 — Strategic Partnerships.__ Long-term, mission-critical relationships, including major technology providers, university accreditation partners, and government funding relationships. These require full due diligence, Board of Stewards approval, and annual review.

__Tier 2 — Operational Partnerships.__ Medium-term relationships supporting day-to-day operations, including curriculum providers, professional development partners, and local service providers. These require due diligence, Chief Executive approval, and bi-annual review.

__Tier 3 — Community Partnerships.__ Local, short-term, or project-based relationships, including a business hosting a workshop visit or a local non-profit for a service project. These require light due diligence and Local Community Leader or Regional Steward approval.

__Tier 4 — Individual Expert Engagements.__ Industry Experts, guest speakers, and mentors. These require background screening per Blueprint 07, mentor approval, and a limited-term engagement scope.

## SECTION FOUR — DUE DILIGENCE PROCESS

Safeguarding is not a separate check appended to this process; it is built into every tier's evaluation from the first step.

__Tier 1 (Strategic).__ A partner background check covering financial stability, reputation, and legal history; a conflict-of-interest check against every Board member and officer; a mission alignment assessment testing the partnership directly against the Vision Statement in Blueprint 01; a constitutional compatibility assessment; a safeguarding assessment examining any history of violations and any risk to learners; a financial terms review; references from at least two other organizations; and, where appropriate, review of the draft agreement by legal counsel.

__Tier 2 (Operational).__ A partner reputation check; a conflict-of-interest check; a mission alignment assessment; a constitutional compatibility assessment; a safeguarding assessment; a financial terms review; and one reference.

__Tier 3 (Community).__ A basic reputation check; a safeguarding check specifically addressing any risk to learners; and terms reviewed by a Community Mentor.

__Tier 4 (Individual Expert).__ Background screening per Blueprint 07; mentor approval; and a scope limited to the specific engagement.

## SECTION FIVE — APPROVAL AUTHORITY

__Tier 1__ requires Board of Stewards approval, with Constitutional Council review for any partnership carrying a significant expansion or constitutional implication, per Part Four below.

__Tier 2__ requires Chief Executive approval, with notification to the Board's Governance & Nominating Committee.

__Tier 3__ requires approval from the Regional Steward or Chief Community Officer responsible for the relevant community.

__Tier 4__ requires mentor approval, with notification to the Regional Steward.

## SECTION SIX — PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT REQUIREMENTS

Every partnership agreement, regardless of tier, includes: a statement of purpose and scope; term and renewal provisions; financial terms, where any exist; safeguarding and child protection commitments, per Blueprint 07; data privacy commitments; an explicit clause stating that no provision of the agreement may compromise DGENIE's constitutional integrity; a termination clause covering both cause and convenience, per Section Eight; a dispute resolution mechanism; and reporting and review provisions scaled to the tier.

## SECTION SEVEN — MONITORING AND REVIEW

__Tier 1__ receives an annual formal review by the Board of Stewards, with a mid-year check-in. __Tier 2__ receives a bi-annual review by the Chief Executive, with an annual check-in. __Tier 3__ receives an annual review by the Regional Steward or Chief Community Officer. __Tier 4__ is reviewed at the end of the engagement, with feedback logged for future reference.

## SECTION EIGHT — TERMINATION

__For cause,__ a partnership is terminated immediately upon notice. __For convenience,__ a partnership ends after the notice period defined in the agreement. __Material constitutional violation__ triggers immediate termination regardless of the agreement's own terms, with the matter reviewed by the Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09.

Every termination includes a termination plan ensuring the orderly transition of any affected services, the protection of learner data, and clear communication to any family affected by the change.

## SECTION NINE — CONFLICT OF INTEREST

Any person involved in a partnership decision who holds a personal, financial, or relational interest in the prospective or existing partner discloses it, applying the Conflict of Interest process already established in Part Two, Section Ten rather than a new one. A person with a disclosed conflict recuses themselves from the decision entirely.

## SECTION TEN — TRANSPARENCY AND DISCLOSURE

Every Tier 1 and Tier 2 partnership is disclosed in the Annual Constitutional Report, per Blueprint 09. Disclosure includes the partner's name, the purpose of the relationship, its term, and its financial significance, where any exists. No partnership is kept secret from the community.

## SECTION ELEVEN — SAFEGUARDING INTEGRATION

Every partnership, at every tier, is evaluated for safeguarding implications as an integral part of due diligence. Partner staff who interact with learners undergo the same background screening required of DGENIE's own staff, per Blueprint 07. The Chief Safeguarding Officer holds the authority to pause or terminate any partnership found to pose a safeguarding risk, immediately and independent of the tier's ordinary approval or termination process.

## SECTION TWELVE — RELATIONSHIP WITH EXPANSION GOVERNANCE

Any partnership constituting a significant expansion requires joint approval from the Board of Stewards and the Constitutional Council, applying the Expansion Governance process established in Blueprint 08 and Part Four below.

## SECTION THIRTEEN — AMENDMENT OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Amendments to this Part require a proposal from the Chief Executive and approval by the Board of Stewards, applying the Framework Amendments tier of the Amendment Process established in Blueprint 08.

# PART FOUR — EXPANSION & LICENSING FRAMEWORK

## SECTION ONE — PURPOSE & SCOPE

This Part governs every form of expansion DGENIE undertakes: Physical Schools, Homeschool Networks, Partner Schools, Universities, Corporate Learning, Church Training, Community Development, Government Partnerships, and International Federations, the nine contexts established in Blueprint 08. It also governs licensing, franchising, and accreditation partnerships.

## SECTION TWO — FOUNDATIONAL EXPANSION PRINCIPLES

Mission precedes growth; expansion serves the Vision established in Blueprint 01, never the reverse. Constitutional integrity is non-negotiable; no expansion may require weakening any Foundational Principle. Quality precedes scale; expansion occurs only at a pace the institution's people, systems, and character can actually sustain. Learner protection is paramount; every expansion is evaluated for its impact on learner safety and dignity before anything else is considered. Formation precedes information; the Graduate Profile and Formation Framework remain unchanged regardless of context. The Genius Level system remains unchanged; the same measurement applies everywhere DGENIE operates. Community is preserved; expansion strengthens, never fragments, the Global Community established in Blueprint 06.

## SECTION THREE — TYPES OF EXPANSION

__Type A, Contextual Expansion,__ applies DGENIE's methodology to a new context among the nine named below. This requires Board of Stewards approval and Constitutional Council review, per Section Four.

The nine expansion contexts are: __Physical Schools,__ where a campus becomes a very large and permanent Local Learning Circle; __Homeschool Networks,__ structurally identical to the existing Home Track; __Partner Schools,__ where an existing school licenses the Curriculum and Methodology while its own staff become Teachers and Mentors bound by the Teacher Creed; __Universities,__ where the Genius Level system extends naturally past Level Twelve; __Corporate Learning,__ where the Five Lifelong Competencies and Contribution Principle apply to adult professional formation; __Church Training,__ where the Spiritual Formation dimension and the Mentor's spiritual-formation responsibility, per Blueprint 11, extend directly; __Community Development,__ where the Local Learning Circle and Community Impact Project structures extend into neighborhoods with no formal learners yet; __Government Partnerships,__ operating at the National Community layer; and __International Federations,__ operating at the Global Community layer, under one Vision and one Community Declaration.

__Type B, Partnership Expansion,__ enters a partnership with an external organization. This is governed by Part Three above in full; this Part does not duplicate that process, only confirms that any partnership rising to the level of an expansion also requires Board of Stewards approval and Constitutional Council review on top of Part Three's tiered due diligence.

__Type C, International Expansion,__ enters a new nation or region. National and Regional Community layers are established, per Blueprint 06. This requires joint Board of Stewards and Constitutional Council approval, per Blueprint 08.

__Type D, Licensing and Franchising,__ licenses the DGENIE brand, curriculum, or methodology to an external entity. Licensed entities adhere to the same constitutional standards as DGENIE itself. This requires Board of Stewards approval and Constitutional Council review, and every licensing agreement is reviewed annually.

## SECTION FOUR — EXPANSION APPROVAL AUTHORITY

__Contextual Expansion__ requires Board of Stewards approval and Constitutional Council review. __Partnership Expansion__ requires the same, applying Part Three's due diligence first. __International Expansion__ requires joint Board of Stewards and Constitutional Council approval. __Licensing and Franchising__ requires Board of Stewards approval and Constitutional Council review.

__Regional Expansion,__ growth within an already-approved nation, rests with Regional Steward authority, with notification to the Board. __Local Expansion,__ growth within an already-approved region, rests with Local Community Leader authority, with notification to the Regional Steward.

__Emergency Expansion,__ responding to a genuine crisis or urgent opportunity, rests with Chief Executive authority for thirty days, with immediate Board notification and mandatory retroactive Board and Constitutional Council approval before the thirtieth day closes. An emergency expansion that does not receive retroactive approval is wound down, not quietly continued.

## SECTION FIVE — EXPANSION DECISION FRAMEWORK

Before any expansion is approved, four sets of questions are answered, in order, and a failure on Mission Fidelity ends the evaluation before the remaining questions are even asked.

__Mission Fidelity:__ does this expansion serve the Vision established in Blueprint 01; does it align with the Graduate Profile; does it preserve Blueprint 03's Formation Framework; does it preserve Blueprint 02's Genius Discovery Framework.

__Constitutional Integrity:__ does this expansion require weakening any Foundational Principle or Constitutional Red Line; does it preserve the Separation of Authority established in Blueprint 08; does it preserve the AI Governance Principles in Blueprint 04 and Blueprint 14; does it preserve the safeguarding standards established in Blueprint 07.

__Quality and Sustainability:__ can DGENIE sustain this expansion without compromising quality; are the people, systems, and resources actually in place to support it; is there a defined transition and integration plan; is there a defined exit plan if the expansion fails.

__Community Impact:__ does this expansion strengthen the Global Community established in Blueprint 06; does it serve the local community it enters; does it preserve the layered belonging structure in Blueprint 06.

__Financial Stewardship:__ is the expansion financially sustainable; does it align with Part Two above; does it avoid the compromising dependence named in Part One, Section Four.

## SECTION SIX — EXPANSION DUE DILIGENCE PROCESS

Due diligence is conducted by the Chief Executive, with input from the Chief Educational Architect on educational integrity; the Chief Technology Architect on technical feasibility; the Chief Safeguarding Officer on learner protection; the Chief Community Officer on community impact; and the Finance & Audit Committee on financial sustainability, per Part Two.

The due diligence report is submitted to the Board of Stewards and includes the expansion rationale, a constitutional assessment against Section Five, a risk analysis, financial projections, and an implementation timeline.

For any expansion classified as a Type B Partnership Expansion, the tiered due diligence process established in Part Three is applied in full; this Section's report requirements sit alongside, not in place of, that process.

## SECTION SEVEN — QUALITY ASSURANCE AND STANDARDS

Every expansion maintains the same standards as the original DGENIE model. Quality assurance mechanisms include regular audits, per Blueprint 09; learner outcome monitoring, tracked as Genius Level progression; community satisfaction measurement; and safeguarding compliance review.

## SECTION EIGHT — BRAND AND IDENTITY PROTECTION

The DGENIE brand, name, logo, and identity are protected assets, per Part One, Section Five's treatment of intellectual property as a resource serving the mission. Licensed entities adhere to defined brand guidelines. Misuse of the DGENIE brand is subject to legal action.

## SECTION NINE — RELATIONSHIP WITH THE GLOBAL COMMUNITY

Expansion strengthens, never fragments, the Global Community established in Blueprint 06. Every new community is integrated into the Global Community structure rather than operating as an isolated satellite.

## SECTION TEN — RELATIONSHIP WITH THE PARENT COMMUNITY COUNCIL

Expansion into new regions creates the conditions the Regional and Global Parent Assembly layers of the Parent Community Council, established in Blueprint 12, depend on; those layers cannot exist without the regional and global structures this Part governs. In this sense, this Part does not merely permit the Parent Framework's full implementation; it is the mechanism that makes it possible.

## SECTION ELEVEN — MONITORING AND REVIEW

Each expansion is reviewed annually by the Chief Executive. A formal review is conducted after the first year of operation. The Oversight Body established in Blueprint 09 may audit expansion practices as part of its Constitutional Audit.

## SECTION TWELVE — TERMINATION AND EXIT

An expansion may be terminated if it no longer serves the mission; violates constitutional integrity; fails to maintain the quality standards established in Section Seven; or creates unacceptable risk to learners or the community. Every termination includes a transition plan protecting learners, families, and community members. Termination of any expansion is approved by the Board of Stewards.

## SECTION THIRTEEN — AMENDMENT OF THIS FRAMEWORK

Amendments to this Part require a proposal from the Chief Executive and approval by the Board of Stewards, applying the Framework Amendments tier of the Amendment Process established in Blueprint 08.
